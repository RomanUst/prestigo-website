## How far is Český Krumlov from Prague?

Český Krumlov sits **175 kilometres south of Prague**, in South Bohemia near the Austrian border. The drive runs down the modern D3 for most of the way, giving a realistic **2 hour 15 minute** door-to-door time in normal traffic. Leave central Prague after the morning rush and you are walking under the castle tower before the tour coaches arrive.

By public transport the same distance stretches out. The coach takes around three hours between terminals, and the train is slower again, usually requiring a change at České Budějovice and leaving you a walk from the station to the old town.

## What to see in Český Krumlov

The town is compact and made for walking. The essentials:

- **Český Krumlov Castle** — the second-largest castle complex in the country, with the instantly recognisable round, painted **castle tower**, terraced gardens, and views over the red rooftops. Climb the tower for the classic photograph of the river bend.
- **The Baroque Theatre** — one of the best-preserved 18th-century theatres in the world, complete with original stage machinery, shown by guided tour only.
- **The Cloak Bridge (Plášťový most)** — the tiered covered bridge linking the castle wings, spanning the moat below.
- **The old town square (Náměstí Svornosti)** and the lanes around it — Renaissance and Baroque facades, small galleries, and the Egon Schiele Art Centrum, a nod to the painter who worked here.
- **The Vltava river** — quiet riverside walks, and in summer the town's famous gentle rafting and canoe trips.

## How long do you need?

You can see the headline sights in a **half day**, but Český Krumlov is a place that rewards lingering — a proper lunch, the castle tower, a slow wander after the day-trippers leave. We recommend planning a **full day**, which a private car makes effortless: you set the departure and return, rather than the four-hour window a group tour allows.

That flexibility is the quiet advantage of a chauffeur. Arrive early to have the lanes almost to yourself, stay for an unhurried lunch, and drive back in the evening light — a rhythm no fixed coach timetable can match.

## The stop worth making: České Budějovice

One advantage of your own car is that the road becomes part of the trip. Many guests ask for a short stop in **České Budějovice**, about 30 minutes before Krumlov — the home of the original Budweiser Budvar brewery and one of the largest historic squares in Central Europe. With a chauffeur it is simply a request; on a coach it is impossible. The nearby château of **Hluboká nad Vltavou**, a white neo-Gothic fantasy, is another popular add-on for a full day out.

## Arriving in Český Krumlov: the practical detail

The historic core of Český Krumlov is a **pedestrian zone**, with vehicle access restricted and parking arranged around the edges.
