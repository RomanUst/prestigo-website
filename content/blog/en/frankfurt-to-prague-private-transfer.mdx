## Pickup at Frankfurt Airport: terminals and meeting point

Frankfurt is now a three-terminal airport, and knowing which one you land at matters for the meeting point.

- **Terminal 1** (halls A, B and C) is home to **Lufthansa** and most of its Star Alliance partners, including **United** and **Air Canada**.
- **Terminal 3** (piers G, H and J) opened in **April 2026**. Over the spring, dozens of airlines moved in from Terminal 2, among them **American Airlines**, **Delta**, **Emirates**, **Qatar Airways** and **Cathay Pacific**.
- **Terminal 2** (halls D and E) still serves a smaller group of carriers.

The free **SkyLine** train links all three terminals in about 8–10 minutes, but you should not need it. Your chauffeur meets you **in the public arrivals area of the terminal you land at**, holding a name board, once you have cleared passport control and collected your bags. Arriving from outside the Schengen area, you go through immigration at Frankfurt. There is no further passport desk in Prague, only the possible spot check at the land border.

## The route: A3 to Nuremberg, A6 to the border, D5 to Prague

The drive falls into three easy sections.

1. **Frankfurt to Nuremberg, about 225 km.** The **A3** runs east from the airport through the wooded Spessart hills and past Würzburg into Franconia. It is one of Germany's busiest motorways, and most of the variation in drive time comes from here.
2. **Nuremberg to the border, about 115 km.** At the Nuremberg interchange the **A6** heads east through the Upper Palatinate to **Waidhaus**, the last German town before the Czech Republic.
3. **Rozvadov to Prague, about 160 km.** Over the border the road becomes the Czech **D5**. It passes Plzeň and reaches Prague from the south-west, entering the city near Zličín. Hotels in Old Town, Malá Strana or Vinohrady are 20–30 minutes from there.

German motorways are toll-free for cars, and the Czech motorway vignette is included in your fare. Your chauffeur checks traffic before departure and takes an alternative if the A3 is blocked. Friday afternoons out of Frankfurt and the start of German school holidays are the slowest times, and can add 30–45 minutes.

## Stops worth making on the way

Three stops sit naturally on the route and turn the transfer into part of the trip.

### Würzburg: an hour from the airport

About **120 km** from FRA, Würzburg sits just off the A3. The **Würzburg Residenz**, a UNESCO-listed baroque palace with Tiepolo's great ceiling fresco above the staircase, is the reason to stop. An hour is enough for the palace and its garden. It is the best break if you landed early and want to stretch your legs.

### Nuremberg: lunch at the halfway point

Nuremberg is the natural lunch stop, almost exactly halfway. Your chauffeur can drop you below the **Kaiserburg**, the castle on the sandstone ridge, and collect you at the Hauptmarkt after you have walked through the old town. From late November to 24 December the square holds the **Christkindlesmarkt**, one of Germany's most famous Christmas markets. Our [Christmas markets guide](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-christmas-markets-chauffeur-2026) has the dates and how to fit it into a winter trip. From Nuremberg, Prague is about three hours further, the same distance as our [Prague to Nuremberg route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-nuremberg).

### Plzeň: the last hour

Plzeň is less than an hour from Prague and right on the D5. The Pilsner Urquell brewery tour or a walk around the main square makes a good late-afternoon stop. See the [Prague to Plzeň route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-plzen) if you would rather come back for a separate day.

**Rothenburg ob der Tauber**, the walled medieval town, is also possible, but it lies south of the A3 and adds roughly an hour and a half of driving in total, plus your time there. It works better as an overnight than as a stop on a jet-lagged arrival day.

## Private car, train or connecting flight?

- **Connecting flight.** Frankfurt to Prague is about an hour in the air, and often the fastest choice on paper. Once you add the minimum connection time at FRA and baggage claim at Prague, the gain over the drive is smaller than it looks. A missed connection after a long-haul delay can also cost you most of the day.
- **Train.** There is **no direct train** between Frankfurt and Prague. Rail journeys typically take **six to seven hours** with at least one change, usually via Nuremberg or Dresden. It is a good budget option if you travel light.
- **Private car.** About five and a half hours, door to door. No second check-in, no changes with suitcases, and the chance to sleep off the overnight flight in the back seat. It is the strongest option for families, groups of three or more, and anyone with more than a carry-on.

## Which car for Frankfurt to Prague

On a five-hour drive the car matters more than on a city transfer.

- **Mercedes-Benz E-Class**: up to 3 passengers and **2 large bags**. Right for a business traveller or a couple travelling light.
- **Mercedes-Benz S-Class**: the same capacity, with a quieter, roomier rear cabin. The choice if you want to sleep after a night flight or work on the way.
- **Mercedes-Benz V-Class**: up to **6 passengers and 6 large bags**. The car for families, small groups, ski bags and long-haul luggage.

## Practical tips for the Frankfurt to Prague transfer

- **Keep passports in the cabin.** Germany has run checks at its land borders since September 2024 and has extended them to at least **15 March 2027**. Most cars pass Waidhaus without stopping, but officers do spot checks, and non-EU passengers must have their passport ready.
- **Book a few days ahead.** We drive to Frankfurt from Prague for your pickup, so we need your flight details a few days in advance.
- **Winter driving.** From November to March, snow is possible in the hills around the border. Our cars are winter-equipped, and the chauffeur will tell you if the forecast calls for extra time.
