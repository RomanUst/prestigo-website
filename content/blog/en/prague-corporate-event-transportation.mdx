## Start with a movement schedule

Before you think about cars, write down every movement: who travels, from where, to where, and when. For a two-day conference it is usually a short list:

1. **Arrivals:** each flight or train, with passenger names and number of bags.
2. **Hotel to venue** on each morning, and back in the evening.
3. **Off-programme moves:** a board dinner, a client visit, a site visit outside Prague.
4. **Departures:** each flight, worked back to a hotel pickup time.

Send us this list, even in rough form, and we turn it into a timed schedule with a car and a chauffeur against every line. That schedule is what we and you then work from. When something changes, we change it there and confirm the update to you.

## How many cars does your delegation need?

Count luggage, not just seats. A V-Class seats six, but six people each with a large suitcase, a cabin bag and a box of conference materials will fill it to the roof. For arrival and departure days, we plan like this:

- **1–3 people:** one E-Class, or an S-Class for a CEO, board member or keynote speaker.
- **4–6 people:** one V-Class, so the team arrives together with their bags.
- **7–12 people:** two V-Class, or one V-Class plus an S-Class for the most senior guest.
- **13–30 people:** several V-Class running in waves, matched to flight times rather than all leaving together.
- **Up to 50 people:** a coordinated V-Class fleet with a lead dispatcher. Above that, a coach is usually the better choice for the bulk movement, with our cars kept for VIPs and late arrivals.

On the conference days themselves, when people travel without luggage, the same group needs fewer vehicles. We plan those separately so you are not paying for empty seats.

## Airport arrivals: waves, not one big pickup

International delegates rarely land together. A typical Prague conference sees guests arriving over a day and a half, at both terminals: **Terminal 1** for flights from outside the Schengen area, including the UK, the US, the Gulf and Asia, and **Terminal 2** for Schengen flights. We build arrivals in waves:

- **Each flight gets its own pickup.** Your chauffeur tracks it live and waits in the arrivals hall with a name board, so an early or delayed landing does not affect anyone else.
- **Shared rides only where flights land close together**, usually within 30–40 minutes, with the earlier passengers told in advance that they will wait briefly.
- **VIPs are met separately.** For a keynote speaker or a board member we can add the airport's fast-track and lounge services; our guide to [Prague Airport meet and greet](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-airport-meet-and-greet) explains what is available at each terminal.

Up to 60 minutes of waiting after landing is included, which covers most passport queues and baggage delays. Details of the airport side are on our [airport transfer page](https://rideprestigo.com/services/airport-transfer).

## Prague's main congress venues and how traffic reaches them

Most large corporate events in Prague take place in a handful of districts. Drive times below are typical daytime figures from Václav Havel Airport:

- **Prague Congress Centre (Kongresové centrum Praha), Prague 4.** Above Vyšehrad metro station, at the southern end of the Nusle Bridge. About 28 minutes from the airport. The bridge carries the city's main north–south road, so morning arrivals between 07:30 and 09:00 need an extra 10 minutes, and we set the drop-off at the venue's entrance rather than the street.
- **O2 universum, Prague 9 (Vysočany).** Next to the O2 arena on Českomoravská, with its own metro and tram stops. Roughly 30 minutes from the airport. When an arena event runs the same evening, the surrounding streets fill early, so we plan departures before the crowd arrives, not after.
- **Karlín, Prague 8.** Forum Karlín and many of the city's newer offices and business hotels. About 32 minutes from the airport, 10 minutes from the Old Town.
- **Old Town, Malá Strana and the river hotels.** Many gala dinners and board meetings are held here. The historic core is largely closed to traffic, so the car stops at the nearest legal point, never more than a short walk from the door. We check the exact drop-off point for every address in advance.
- **Pankrác and Smíchov.** The two main business districts, for client meetings between sessions.

## On the event day: timing, waiting and the dispatcher

Conference days rarely run to time. Sessions overrun, a dinner moves by half an hour, someone needs to leave early for a flight. Three things keep the transport running smoothly:

- **One dispatcher with the full schedule.** You, your event manager or your assistant call one number, not individual chauffeurs. The dispatcher reallocates cars when the programme moves.
- **Cars on standby rather than on call.** For the hours around the main session breaks and the evening programme, we can keep one or two cars waiting at the venue, so an early departure or an unwell guest is handled in minutes.
- **Hourly booking for executives.** A board member who will be moving between the venue, client offices and a dinner is usually best served by one car and one chauffeur for the day. See [car with driver in Prague by the hour](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/car-with-driver-prague-by-the-hour) for how that works.

Chauffeurs speak English and treat passenger names and itineraries as confidential. For sensitive meetings, we can issue the schedule with initials instead of names on request.

## Frequently asked questions

**How far in advance should we book transport for a Prague conference?**
Two to four weeks for a delegation of up to 30 people. For the autumn congress season and December, when Christmas events fill the city's cars and hotels, book as soon as you have flight details.

**How many people fit in a Mercedes V-Class?**
Up to six passengers with six large bags. On conference days without luggage, it is the most efficient car for moving small teams between the hotel and the venue.

**Can you meet guests arriving on different flights?**
Yes. Each flight gets its own pickup and chauffeur, tracked live, with up to 60 minutes of waiting after landing included.

**Do you provide a dispatcher during the event?**
Yes. One dispatcher holds the full movement schedule and is reachable by phone and WhatsApp throughout the event, and reallocates cars when the programme changes.

**How long is the drive from Prague Airport to the Prague Congress Centre?**
About 28 minutes in typical daytime traffic. Allow an extra 10 minutes between 07:30 and 09:00 on weekdays, when the Nusle Bridge is busiest.

**Can we pay with one invoice for the whole event?**
Yes. We quote the event as a whole and issue one invoice, or bill it through a corporate account with one monthly invoice.

**Can you transport VIP guests separately from the group?**
Yes. VIPs can travel in an S-Class with their own chauffeur, with airport fast-track added on request, while the rest of the delegation travels by V-Class.
