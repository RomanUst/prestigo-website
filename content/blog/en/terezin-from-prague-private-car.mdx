## What Terezín was

Terezín was founded in the 1780s by Emperor Joseph II as a fortress against Prussia: a walled garrison town and, across the river, a smaller fortress. Neither ever saw a siege.

During the Second World War the Nazis turned both into instruments of persecution. The **Small Fortress** became a Gestapo police prison, where some 32,000 men and women, most of them Czech political prisoners, were held between 1940 and 1945. The walled town became the **Theresienstadt ghetto**. About 140,000 Jews from Bohemia, Moravia, Germany, Austria and other countries were deported there; tens of thousands died in the town, and most of the rest were sent on to Auschwitz and other camps in the east. The ghetto was also the place the Nazis staged for a Red Cross visit in 1944 and filmed for propaganda, while the transports went on.

Today the Terezín Memorial cares for the sites, the cemeteries and the archives. It is a place of remembrance, and visitors come with that in mind.

## How a half day at Terezín works by car

The order that works for most visitors, and the one the Memorial suggests for a half day, is the Small Fortress first and the town second.

**1. The National Cemetery and the Small Fortress.** The car parks about 200 metres from the fortress gate. In front of it lies the National Cemetery, with some 2,400 individual graves and the remains of around 10,000 victims. Inside, the route leads through the prison courtyards, the cells, the showers and the sick room, the execution ground and a long fortification corridor. On your own, allow about 90 minutes; a guided tour takes about 70 minutes, and the museum and the documentary in the fortress cinema add a little more.

**2. Into the town.** Your chauffeur drives you the kilometre into Terezín, to the car park by the information centre or the Crematorium. The former **Magdeburg Barracks** was the seat of the ghetto's Jewish self-government. Its exhibitions cover music, art, literature and theatre in the ghetto, there is a reconstructed dormitory, and for now it also holds the temporary exhibition on the ghetto's history. Allow about 45 minutes.

**3. The Crematorium and the cemeteries.** Just outside the town walls stand the **Crematorium** and the **Jewish Cemetery**, about 600 metres from the Magdeburg Barracks. Nearby, inside the old fortifications, are the **Columbarium**, the ceremonial halls and the central morgue, and in Dlouhá street the small **prayer room** from the ghetto years. Each takes 15 to 20 minutes.

That is a full and unhurried visit. Add the drive, and the whole trip from your hotel door and back is about five to six hours.

## Guides, tickets and the audio guide

**Memorial guides.** Only guides employed or authorised by the Terezín Memorial may lead visitors inside the Small Fortress. The Memorial's own tour of the fortress runs in English, German, Italian, Spanish and Czech, and is included in the ticket. Book it in advance through the [reservation form on the Memorial's website](https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/reservation) or by email; a member of staff confirms the time. Individual visitors may be joined with others, and a private tour can be requested but is not guaranteed. While the Ghetto Museum is closed, the guided route through the former ghetto is suspended, and the town sites are visited on your own.

**Tickets.** One ticket covers the Small Fortress and the former ghetto sites, is valid for four days and lets you enter each site once. It is sold at the Small Fortress and in the Magdeburg Barracks, by card or cash. Children under ten enter free.

**Audio guide and printed guide.** The Memorial offers an audio guide for phones and a printed guidebook in many languages, sold on site. Either works well for the town sites.

Your chauffeur drives and waits but does not guide. If you book a Memorial tour, tell us the time and we plan the pickup in Prague around it.

## Opening days and the best time to go

The Memorial's summer hours run from April to October, the winter hours from November to March.

- **Small Fortress:** daily, 9:00–17:00 in summer and 9:00–16:00 in winter.
- **Magdeburg Barracks and the prayer room:** daily, 9:00–17:00 all year.
- **Crematorium, Columbarium, ceremonial halls and morgue:** 10:00–17:00 in summer and 10:00–16:00 in winter, **closed on Saturdays**.
- **Christmas and New Year:** all sites close at 15:00 on 23 and 31 December and are closed on 24, 25 and 26 December and on 1 January.

A weekday morning is the calmest time. Leaving Prague around 8:30 puts you at the Small Fortress soon after it opens, before most coach groups arrive, and leaves the town sites for late morning when the Crematorium is open. If you travel on a Saturday, see the Small Fortress and the Magdeburg Barracks, and know that the Crematorium will be closed.

## Etiquette, children and accessibility

The Memorial asks visitors to keep quiet, stay on the marked route and not eat, drink or smoke inside the buildings. Photography for personal use is free, but not with flash, tripods or selfie sticks indoors, and not where signs or staff say otherwise; recording a guide's talk is not allowed. There is no dress code beyond avoiding clothing with political or offensive messages.

The Memorial publishes no minimum age. Families do visit, often with teenagers studying the war at school, but some exhibitions describe suffering and death plainly. Parents usually decide by the child's age and readiness.

All sites are accessible to wheelchair users, though the Small Fortress route, the Crematorium, the Columbarium and the central morgue need a little help in places. The fortress museum, cinema and toilets are step-free. The car brings you close to each area, and a V-Class has room for a folding wheelchair.

## Frequently asked questions

**How far is Terezín from Prague?**
About 60 km north, roughly 50 minutes by car on the D8 motorway. The Memorial's car parks are a few minutes from the motorway exit.

**How long do we need at the Terezín Memorial?**
For the Small Fortress and the main ghetto sites, two to four hours. The Small Fortress alone takes about 90 minutes on your own, or about 70 minutes with a Memorial guide.

**Is the Ghetto Museum open?**
It has been closed for reconstruction since 2 November 2026. A temporary exhibition on the history of the ghetto is on show in the former Magdeburg Barracks. Check the Memorial's website for the reopening date.

**Can our chauffeur guide us at Terezín?**
No. Your chauffeur drives and waits. Inside the Small Fortress only Memorial guides or guides it authorises may lead visitors. Book a Memorial tour in advance and we plan the day around it.

**Is the Terezín Memorial open on Saturdays?**
The Small Fortress and the Magdeburg Barracks are open every day. The Crematorium, the Columbarium and the ceremonial halls are closed on Saturdays.

**Can we visit Terezín in winter?**
Yes. The sites are open all year, with the Small Fortress and the Crematorium closing at 16:00 from November to March. The Memorial is closed on 24–26 December and on 1 January.

**Is Terezín suitable for children?**
There is no official minimum age, and children under ten enter free. Some exhibitions are hard to see, so parents decide by the child's age. Child seats in our cars are free.

**Can we combine Terezín with Litoměřice or Dresden?**
Yes. Litoměřice is minutes away and suits lunch after the visit. Dresden is on the same motorway, so a stop at Terezín fits a transfer from Prague to Dresden.
