## How the Prague airport pickup works, from landing to the car

**Give your flight number when you book.** It is more useful than the airline name: the chauffeur tracks that exact flight and knows in advance which terminal you will come out of.

**Your terminal depends on where you connect.** Most flights from Asia to Prague involve a connection. If you change planes inside the Schengen area (Frankfurt, Munich or Vienna, for example), you clear passport control there and usually arrive at **Terminal 2** in Prague. If you connect through a hub outside Schengen (Dubai, Doha or Istanbul, for example), you go through passport control in **Terminal 1**. The two terminals are a five-minute indoor walk apart, and the chauffeur waits in whichever arrivals hall you actually use. Terminal-by-terminal detail is in our [Prague airport arrivals guide](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-airport-arrivals-guide).

**Flight tracking and 60 minutes of free waiting.** Delays on long connecting journeys are common. The pickup time follows your actual landing, and the 60 minutes of free waiting after touchdown cover the passport queue, a slow baggage belt or even a lost-luggage report without any rush.

**Look for your name after customs.** The chauffeur stands at the arrivals exit with a board showing your name, takes your luggage and walks you to the car. ## Airport to the city: private car versus public transport

Václav Havel Airport lies about 17 km north-west of the centre, and **there is currently no direct rail link into the city**. There are three usual ways in:

- **Private car:** door to door, usually 25–35 minutes depending on traffic and your hotel. Luggage goes straight into the boot and you step out at the hotel entrance.
- **Trolleybus 59 plus metro line A:** line 59 (which replaced bus 119 in 2024) takes about 15 minutes to Nádraží Veleslavín, where you change to metro line A. Tickets must be bought before boarding, and changing with large suitcases is hard work.
- **AE Airport Express:** runs to Prague main railway station (Hlavní nádraží), convenient if you are staying nearby or continuing by train the same day.

For a solo traveller with light bags, public transport works perfectly well. After a long-haul journey with two large suitcases, older parents or children, or a hotel on a pedestrian street in the Old Town, a door-to-door car removes the hardest stretch of the day.

## Choosing the car: E-Class, S-Class or V-Class

- **Mercedes E-Class:** up to 3 passengers; suits one or two travellers with two large suitcases and two cabin bags.
- **Mercedes S-Class:** also up to 3 passengers, with more rear space and a quieter cabin, for business travel or a special occasion.
- **Mercedes V-Class:** up to 6 passengers with luggage, for families, two or three couples together, or long trips with plenty of bags.

A simple rule: **more than three people, or one large checked suitcase each, means a V-Class.** State passengers and luggage when you book and we will assign the right car. Travelling with children, add each child's age and approximate weight, and the infant seat, child seat or booster will already be fitted. After the pickup, the same service can continue as your charter. In Prague it is used in two ways.

**By the hour (2-hour minimum).** Ideal for a day with several stops: start the morning at the upper entrance of Prague Castle and walk downhill to Malá Strana while the chauffeur meets you at the bottom for lunch; shop on Pařížská in the afternoon with bags going straight into the car; go to a performance at the National Theatre in the evening and find the car at the door when it ends. The chauffeur waits at every stop, and you extend simply by telling them. The details are in our guide to [hiring a car with driver in Prague by the hour](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/car-with-driver-prague-by-the-hour).

**Day trips near Prague.** The two most popular destinations both suit a private car:

- **Kutná Hora:** about 70 km east, around 1 hour each way. The Sedlec Ossuary, St Barbara's Cathedral and a UNESCO old town fit comfortably into half a day, leaving the afternoon in Prague.
- **Český Krumlov:** about 175 km south, around 2.5 hours each way. A UNESCO town wrapped in a bend of the Vltava; with a car you can arrive before the coach groups, leave after they have gone and stop in České Budějovice on the way.

## FAQ

**What do the Chinese searches 布拉格機場接送 and 布拉格包車 mean?**
They are the traditional-Chinese terms used by travellers from Taiwan and Hong Kong. 機場接送 is a door-to-door car between Václav Havel Airport and your hotel; 包車 is a car with a chauffeur hired by the hour or by the day. Both are booked on the same page.

**Where will the chauffeur meet me at Prague airport?**
At the arrivals exit of the terminal you actually land in, holding a board with your name. Flights connecting outside Schengen usually arrive at Terminal 1; flights connecting inside Schengen usually arrive at Terminal 2.

**What if my flight is delayed?**
Nothing to do. We track the flight live, the pickup follows the actual landing time, and 60 minutes of free waiting start after touchdown.

Usually 25–35 minutes door to door, about 17 km, depending on traffic and your hotel.

**How many people fit in a V-Class?**

**Do the chauffeurs speak Chinese?**

**What is the minimum for an hourly charter?**
Two hours, with no upper limit; day trips and cross-border routes can be booked directly by destination.

**Can I hire a car from Prague to Vienna or Budapest?**
Yes. Vienna is about 3.5 hours and Budapest about 5.5 hours, door to door, with stops arranged along the way.
