The last wave of flights into Prague lands late. Delayed connections, long-haul arrivals from the Gulf and North America, and budget flights with last-minute slots fill the arrivals halls well after midnight. That is when the airport is at its most confusing: the express bus has stopped, the night network runs on a different timetable, the taxi queue is longest, and you have been travelling since morning. This guide covers **late-night arrivals at Prague Airport**: what still runs, the half-hour when nothing does, what happens if your flight is delayed, and how the same logic applies to very early departures.

## Late-night arrival at Prague Airport at a glance

- **Airport:** Václav Havel Airport Prague (PRG). **Terminal 1** handles non-Schengen flights (UK, US, Middle East, long-haul) and **Terminal 2** handles Schengen and EU flights.
- **Distance to the centre:** about **20 km**. At night the drive to Old Town or Vinohrady usually takes **25 minutes or less**.
- **Public transport after midnight:** the night bus **910** runs until about **03:54**. Daytime trolleybus **59** restarts at around **04:22**. Between the two, nothing runs.
- **Official taxi rank:** open 24/7 at both terminals. Late at night it often has the longest queue of the day.
- **Pre-booked chauffeur:** the driver tracks your flight, waits inside Arrivals with a name board, and includes **up to 60 minutes of free waiting** after landing.
- **Price:** one fixed quote agreed before you fly, with any night-hour pricing already included, so nothing is added at the kerb. See our [airport transfer service](https://rideprestigo.com/services/airport-transfer).

The short version: if you land after 23:00 with luggage, children or a hotel that wants you checked in, arrange the transfer before you fly.

## What still runs at Prague Airport after midnight

Prague's airport is busy until late, but the transport around it slows down after 23:00. Here is what to expect:

- **The Airport Express (AE) bus** to the main railway station stops in the evening. It is not an option for a midnight arrival.
- **Night bus 910** takes over from the daytime trolleybus and links the airport to the night network. It works, but it means a connection, a night tram or a walk at the other end, and it only runs about every half hour.
- **The official taxi rank** stays open all night in front of both terminals. After a late long-haul arrival, though, several hundred people can reach the kerb at once.
- **Hotel shuttles** exist at a few airport hotels, but almost none serve city-centre hotels at night.

None of this is unsafe. Prague is a calm city at night. But each option needs decisions at the point when you are least able to make them: which ticket, which stop, which queue, which address to show the driver.

## The gap: 03:54 to 04:22

There is one window each night when **no public transport serves Prague Airport at all**. The last night bus 910 leaves at around 03:54, and trolleybus 59, which replaced the old bus 119, does not start until around 04:22. Delayed flights, especially those that left the Gulf or the US late, often land in or just before this window.

If you land in that window, you have three options: queue at the taxi rank, wait in the terminal for the first trolleybus, or walk out to a driver who is already there. ## Getting into your hotel at 02:00

The airport is only half the problem. The other half is a hotel or apartment at two in the morning. A few habits from our late-night pickups:

- **Tell the hotel you are arriving late.** Most Prague hotels have a 24-hour reception, but boutique hotels and smaller guesthouses sometimes lock the door after midnight. A short email the day before avoids standing on a cobbled street with a suitcase.
- **Apartments and key boxes.** If you are staying in a rental, save the check-in instructions and the door code offline. Your chauffeur can wait while you open the door, and that is standard.
- **Food.** Few kitchens are open at 02:00. Bottled water is in every car, and if you need a 24-hour shop on the way, just ask.
- **Address in writing.** Old Town streets can have similar names. We confirm your exact address when you book, so you will not need to explain it at night.

## The other end of the night: early-morning departures

The same logic applies in reverse. Prague's first departures leave around 06:00, so many travellers leave their hotel at 04:00, when public transport has barely restarted.

Work backwards from your flight:

1. **Your airline's recommended arrival time.** Usually around **two hours** before departure for Schengen flights and up to **three hours** for long-haul. Check your own airline.
2. **The drive:** about **25 minutes** from the centre at that hour.
3. **A small margin:** 10–15 minutes for loading luggage and the early security queue.

For example, for a **06:30 flight to London** (non-Schengen, Terminal 1), aim to be at the airport by about 04:00 and get picked up in Old Town at around **03:30**. For a **07:00 flight to Paris** (Schengen, Terminal 2), aim for about 05:00 and a pickup at around **04:25**.

Your chauffeur is waiting outside the hotel before the agreed time, so you do not need to call a car and wait in the lobby. If you are leaving very early, ask the hotel for a breakfast box the evening before. Most will prepare one.

## Practical tips for landing in Prague late at night

- **Book before you fly**, at least a few hours ahead, and include the **flight number**.
- **Download offline maps** and your accommodation details in case your phone dies or roaming fails.
- **Only take a car that was arranged for you**, either at the official taxi rank or with your pre-booked driver. Ignore anyone who approaches you inside the terminal offering a ride.
- **Keep some euros or a card handy** for tips or small purchases. Your transfer itself is paid in advance.
- **Arriving from outside Schengen?** Allow extra time at Terminal 1 passport control. Your free waiting time is there for that.
