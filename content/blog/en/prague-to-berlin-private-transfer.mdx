Prague and Berlin are two of the great capitals of Central Europe, about 350 kilometres apart. The trip can be a dull motorway run or a short journey in its own right, and the difference is mostly about who controls the schedule. This guide covers the **Prague to Berlin private transfer** by chauffeur: how long the drive takes, the route north through Saxony, the stops worth making in Dresden and Saxon Switzerland, and how drop-offs work in Berlin, including at BER airport.

## Prague to Berlin at a glance

- **Distance:** about **350 km** from central Prague to central Berlin.
- **Drive time:** around **4 hours** door to door in normal traffic, with a coffee stop if you want one.
- **Route:** D8 north past Ústí nad Labem, then the Czech–German border, the A17 around Dresden and the A13 north through Brandenburg into Berlin.
- **Vehicles:** Mercedes-Benz **E-Class** or **S-Class** for up to 3 passengers, **V-Class** for up to 6 passengers and 6 bags.
- **Included:** fuel, the Czech vignette, German motorway costs, driver time and drop-off at any Berlin address or at BER.
- **Price:** one fixed quote for the whole car, agreed before pickup. See the [Prague to Berlin route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-berlin).

In short, it is four hours with a driver who picks you up at your hotel and stops wherever you ask. You can stop once for coffee or turn the drive into a day in Saxony.

## How long does the drive from Prague to Berlin take?

Plan on **about four hours** from a hotel in Old Town, Malá Strana or Vinohrady to a hotel in Mitte or Charlottenburg. The motorway runs the whole way, and most of the time goes to the long A13 stretch through Brandenburg.

Three things can stretch that estimate:

- **Leaving Prague on a Friday afternoon.** Outbound traffic on the D8 can add 20–30 minutes.
- **Roadworks on the A13.** This motorway between Dresden and Berlin has had recurring construction. When a closure is flagged, your chauffeur takes the A4 and A15 instead, and you do not need to say anything.
- **Stops.** A coffee break adds 15–20 minutes. Stopping in Dresden or Saxon Switzerland turns the drive into a half day or a full day, covered below.

**On the train.** The direct EuroCity runs along the Elbe and takes a little over four hours between the main stations. It is a good, scenic option and the honest choice if you travel light and both your hotels are near a station. The car makes sense when you have luggage, children or a group, when you want specific stops, or when you need to reach a door rather than a platform, such as a hotel in the west of Berlin, an office or a departures kerb at BER.

## The route: north through the Elbe valley

Your chauffeur leaves Prague on the **D8 motorway**, heading north across the Bohemian plain towards the volcanic hills of the **České středohoří**. The road climbs past **Lovosice** and **Ústí nad Labem**, over the ridge of the Ore Mountains, and crosses into Saxony.

**About the border.** The Czech Republic and Germany are both in the Schengen Area, but Germany has run temporary checks at its land borders in recent years. Usually that means slowing down through a checkpoint. Now and then there is a short queue. **Keep your passport or national ID** in the cabin with you rather than in the boot.

In Germany the road becomes the **A17**, drops into the Elbe valley and skirts **Dresden**. From there the **A13** runs north through the pine forests and lakes of Brandenburg, passes the Spreewald and reaches the southern edge of Berlin. BER airport sits right on this approach, and the city centre is about 30 minutes further on.

## Stops worth making on the way

Here a private transfer works differently from a train ticket. The route passes some of the best sights in eastern Germany, and you decide how much of that you want.

### Dresden: the obvious stop

Dresden lies about **two hours from Prague**, roughly halfway, and is the natural place to break the trip. Even a 90-minute stop is enough to walk from the **Frauenkirche**, the rebuilt baroque church on the Neumarkt, along the **Brühl's Terrace** above the Elbe to the **Zwinger** and the **Semperoper**. With more time you can add lunch in the old town or a visit to the **Green Vault**, the treasury of the Saxon kings. Book its tickets in advance.

Your chauffeur drops you at the edge of the old town and collects you from the same spot. Your luggage stays locked in the car the whole time.

### Saxon Switzerland and the Bastei Bridge

For travellers with a free day, this is the most memorable detour on the route. **Saxon Switzerland** is a national park of sandstone towers and gorges along the Elbe, just across the border from Bohemian Switzerland on the Czech side. The **Bastei Bridge**, a sandstone bridge from the mid-19th century, crosses the rock pinnacles high above the river. The views over the Elbe valley are among the most photographed in Germany.

The detour adds roughly **an hour of driving**, plus however long you spend on the viewpoints. The walk from the car park to the bridge is short and level. Allow 60–90 minutes on site. Comfortable shoes are worth it, and in winter the paths can be icy.

### The Spreewald: a stop right on the A13

Close to Berlin, the A13 passes the **Spreewald**, a UNESCO biosphere reserve of small canals, forests and old villages. In the warmer months, a punt ride from **Lübbenau** is a calm way to stretch your legs before the city. Year-round, it is a good place for lunch and to try the region's famous gherkins.

## Arriving in Berlin: hotels, stations and BER

- **Hotels anywhere in the city.** Mitte, Charlottenburg, Kreuzberg, Grunewald: your chauffeur drives to the door and helps with luggage.
- **Berlin Hauptbahnhof** for an onward train, if your trip continues north or west.
- **Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER).** BER lies south-east of the city on the A13/A113 approach, so you never enter central Berlin. If you are flying out, give us the flight number and departure time and we will plan a drop-off at the departures level with a comfortable margin.

The trip also works in reverse. Your chauffeur can meet you **inside the arrivals hall at BER** or collect you from your Berlin hotel and drive you to any address in Prague.

## Which car for Prague to Berlin?

Four hours is long enough for the choice of car to matter.

- **Mercedes-Benz E-Class**: up to 3 passengers and **2 large bags**. The right choice for a business traveller or a couple travelling light.
- **Mercedes-Benz S-Class**: the same capacity with a quieter, roomier rear cabin, the better choice if you plan to work, take calls or sleep on the way.
- **Mercedes-Benz V-Class**: up to **6 passengers and 6 bags**. This is the car for families, groups and anyone with sports equipment or a pushchair.

**A simple rule:** if a couple has more than two large suitcases, book the V-Class. Child seats (rear-facing infant, forward-facing toddler or booster) are included at no extra cost. Tell us the children's ages when you book. A German-speaking chauffeur is available on request.
