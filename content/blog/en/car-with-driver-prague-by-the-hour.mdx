Some days in Prague do not fit into a single journey from A to B. A morning of meetings in three districts, an afternoon at the Castle with a lunch reservation on the other side of the river, an evening at the opera followed by dinner across town — each leg is short, but together they add up to a day of waiting for taxis, hunting for addresses and walking cobblestones in the wrong shoes. That is exactly what **hiring a car with driver in Prague by the hour** is for. This guide explains how hourly chauffeur hire works in 2026, when it makes more sense than separate transfers, and what a well-planned day by the hour actually looks like.

## Hourly chauffeur hire in Prague at a glance

- **What it is:** a Mercedes-Benz and a professional chauffeur reserved for you for a set number of hours. The car goes where you go and waits while you are inside.
- **Minimum booking:** **2 hours**, with no upper limit — half a day, a full day, or several days in a row.
- **Vehicles:** **E-Class** or **S-Class** for up to 3 passengers, **V-Class** for up to 6 passengers with luggage.
- **Included:** the chauffeur's time, fuel, waiting between stops, as many stops as you like within the booked hours, bottled water and phone chargers on board.
- **Changes on the day:** extend by telling your chauffeur — no new booking, no app, no dispatch call.
- **Availability:** 24/7, including early mornings and late nights.
- **Price:** a fixed hourly rate confirmed when you book, never a meter — see our [hourly chauffeur service in Prague](https://rideprestigo.com/services/city-rides).

In one line: you decide the plan, and the car and chauffeur follow it for as long as you need.

## Transfer or hourly hire? How to choose

Most journeys in Prague are simple transfers: airport to hotel, hotel to restaurant, station to office. For those, a one-way booking is the natural choice. Hourly hire starts to make sense when the day has **three or more stops close together**, or when there is **waiting in between** that would otherwise mean booking and re-booking cars.

A useful test:

- **One or two journeys with long gaps** (a morning meeting and an evening dinner) — book separate transfers.
- **Several stops within a few hours** (meetings, a viewing, lunch, a shop) — book by the hour. The chauffeur waits outside each stop, so you walk out of one door and into the car.
- **Luggage in play** — a check-out, two meetings and an afternoon flight are much easier when your suitcases stay in the boot all day.
- **Uncertain timings** — negotiations that may run long, a dinner that may become a late evening. With hourly hire nothing needs to be rebooked; the plan simply stretches.

The minimum is two hours, so even a short sequence — a hotel pickup, a viewing in Vinohrady, lunch in the Old Town and back — fits comfortably into a single booking.

## Sample days by the hour

The best way to understand hourly hire is to see how a day flows. These are typical plans our chauffeurs drive; every one can be shortened, stretched or reordered on the day.

### A business day across three districts (4–6 hours)

Pickup at your hotel at 08:30. First meeting in **Karlín**, where many of Prague's newer offices sit along the river. A second meeting in **Pankrác**, the business district south of the centre, then lunch back in the **Old Town** or **Malá Strana**. The afternoon finishes at a client's office near **Náměstí Republiky** — and if your flight leaves that evening, the day ends at the departures kerb of Václav Havel Airport with your luggage already in the car.

Between meetings the rear cabin works as a quiet office: take a call, review documents, reply to email. The chauffeur handles the routes, the one-way streets and the drop-off points, so you arrive at each door on time and unflustered.

### Prague Castle and Malá Strana without the climb (3–4 hours)

Prague Castle sits on a hill above Malá Strana, and the classic walk up is steep and crowded. With a chauffeur, the morning starts at the **Castle's upper entrances**, close to the Strahov side, so you begin at the top and explore downhill: the castle courtyards, St Vitus Cathedral, Golden Lane, then down through the gardens to **Malá Strana**. Your chauffeur meets you at the bottom for a short hop to lunch, or across the river to **Josefov** and the Jewish Quarter. It is the same walk, minus the climb and the backtracking — particularly welcome for older travellers or anyone with young children.

### Shopping on Pařížská and beyond (3 hours)

**Pařížská Street** runs from the Old Town Square towards the river and is home to Prague's luxury boutiques. A shopping afternoon by the hour means bags go straight into the boot after each store, and the next stop — **Na Příkopě**, a jeweller in the New Town, the department stores near **Wenceslas Square** — is a short drive rather than a walk with your hands full. The chauffeur waits nearby while you browse, and the purchases ride back to your hotel with you.

### An evening at the opera or a concert (3–5 hours)

Collected from your hotel at 18:30 for a performance at the **National Theatre**, the **State Opera** or the **Rudolfinum**, then on to dinner. When the curtain falls, the car is outside rather than somewhere in the queue of taxis, and later that night it takes you back to the hotel without any calls. Evening wear, heels and cobblestones are not a natural combination; being dropped at the door is the entire point.

### A family day with a V-Class (6–8 hours)

Families often use hourly hire to cover a full day: the zoo in **Troja** in the morning, lunch, the **Petřín** funicular and gardens, then an early dinner. The V-Class takes up to six passengers, a pushchair and the day bags, and child seats are fitted before pickup. Tired children nap between stops; nobody waits on a tram platform at five o'clock.

## Practical details that make the day work

- **Pedestrian zones.** Large parts of the Old Town and the streets around Charles Bridge are closed to cars. Your chauffeur knows the nearest points where a car may stop and will tell you exactly where to meet after each visit.
- **Where the car waits.** In central Prague the chauffeur often circles or waits in a nearby legal spot rather than outside the door. A short message or call when you are ready, and the car is at the agreed meeting point within minutes.
- **Contact with your chauffeur.** You receive the chauffeur's name, phone number and the vehicle details before pickup, so any change of plan goes directly to the person driving.
- **Extending on the day.** If the meeting runs long or dinner turns into a late evening, simply tell your chauffeur. Extra time is added at the same hourly rate.
- **Dress and discretion.** Chauffeurs wear dark suits, speak English, and keep conversation to what you want. Business discussions in the rear cabin stay there.
- **Child seats.** Infant, toddler and booster seats are available at no extra cost; tell us the ages when you book.
- **Airport at the end of the day.** An hourly booking can finish at Václav Havel Airport.
