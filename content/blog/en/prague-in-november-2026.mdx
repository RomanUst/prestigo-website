**Prague in November** is the quiet month: the summer crowds have gone, the Christmas markets have not yet arrived, and hotels, restaurants and concert halls are easier to book than at any other time of the autumn. Expect cool, grey days around 5 °C, sunset well before five o'clock and a city that turns inward to cafés, wine bars and concerts. This guide covers what visitors from overseas need to plan around in November 2026: the weather and daylight, the few dates that change the city, which castles switch to winter hours, which day trips still make sense and how to arrive and move around comfortably.

## Prague in November at a glance

- **Weather:** highs around 5–8 °C early in the month, closer to 3–5 °C by the end; nights around 0–2 °C. Drizzle is more common than snow.
- **Daylight:** sunrise around 06:55 on 1 November and 07:35 by the 30th; sunset from about 16:40 down to 16:00. Clocks went back on 25 October, so Prague is on CET (UTC+1).
- **Key dates:** All Souls' Day on 2 November, St Martin's Day on Wednesday 11 November, a public holiday on Tuesday 17 November, the Christmas markets from Saturday 28 November.
- **Prague Castle:** winter season from 1 November — historic buildings 09:00–16:00, castle grounds 06:00–22:00.
- **Crowds:** the lowest of the autumn until the last weekend, when the markets open.
- **Day trips:** spa towns, Kutná Hora and Dresden work well; many smaller castles close or open only at weekends.

## What Prague in November weather is really like

November is the dampest-feeling month of the Prague year rather than the coldest. Low cloud often sits over the river valley for days, fog rolls along the Vltava in the mornings and light rain comes and goes. A dusting of snow is possible in the second half of the month, but it rarely settles in the city.

The real constraint is light. With the sun down around 16:15 for most of the month, the useful sightseeing window is roughly 09:00 to 15:30. A good November day in Prague looks like this:

- **Morning outdoors.** Charles Bridge and the Old Town lanes before 10:00 are close to empty, and the fog over the river is at its most photogenic.
- **Midday at the castle.** Arrive by 11:00 so you have time inside the historic buildings before they close at 16:00.
- **Afternoon indoors.** Museums, the Municipal House, a long lunch or a café such as the Slavia opposite the National Theatre.
- **Evening out.** Concerts, opera and dinner, when the city is lit and the cold matters less.

Pack a warm waterproof coat, a scarf and shoes that grip on wet cobbles. Gloves and a hat earn their place on evenings by the river.

## The dates that shape the month

November is short on big events, which is part of its appeal. Four dates are worth knowing in 2026:

- **2 November — All Souls' Day (Dušičky).** Families visit graves over the first weekend of the month, and cemeteries fill with candles after dark. Vyšehrad Cemetery, where Dvořák and Mucha are buried, is quietly moving at dusk. It is a working day, not a holiday.
- **11 November — St Martin's Day.** The first young wine of the year, *svatomartinské víno*, is traditionally opened at 11:11, and restaurants across Prague serve St Martin's goose with red cabbage and dumplings for a week or two around the date. Book a table if you want it on the 11th itself.
- **17 November — Struggle for Freedom and Democracy Day.** A public holiday marking the student protests of 1939 and 1989. People lay candles at the memorial on Národní street and gather on Wenceslas Square. Unlike the Christmas holidays, large shops may stay open; banks and offices close, and public transport runs on a holiday timetable.
- **28 November — the Christmas markets open.** The main markets on Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square open the day before the first Sunday of Advent, 29 November, and the tree is usually lit at dusk on opening day. ## Winter hours: what is open and what is not

From 1 November, Prague's sights move to winter timetables. At Prague Castle, the historic buildings — the Old Royal Palace, St George's Basilica and Golden Lane — open 09:00–16:00, while the courtyards and gardens around them stay open from 06:00 to 22:00. St Vitus Cathedral opens at 12:00 on Sundays because of services, so plan the cathedral for a weekday or a Sunday afternoon.

Outside the city, the change is sharper. Many Czech castles and châteaux end their main season in October and open only at weekends, or close completely, until spring. Karlštejn has in past winters run a single tour from Friday to Sunday, and interior tours at Český Krumlov Castle pause over winter, although the town itself is as beautiful as ever. Before building a day around a castle, check its official site for November dates and book the tour; our [Karlštejn and Konopiště day trip guide](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/karlstejn-konopiste-castle-day-trip-from-prague) explains how the two castles fit together.

Museums, galleries, concert halls and the opera keep their usual schedules, which is why November suits visitors who come for culture rather than open-air sightseeing.

## Day trips that work in November

Short daylight changes the arithmetic of a day trip. A drive of about one to two hours each way leaves enough light at the destination, and the return happens after dark, when a private car is far easier than a cold platform.

- **Karlovy Vary** — about 130 km, roughly 1.5 hours west. The spa colonnades and hot springs are open all year, and the thermal pools of the spa hotels are at their best on a raw November day. See the [Prague to Karlovy Vary route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-karlovy-vary).
- **Mariánské Lázně** — about 165 km, roughly 2 hours west. Quieter and greener than Karlovy Vary, with a singing fountain in the main colonnade. Combine the two spa towns only if you leave Prague early.
- **Kutná Hora** — about 70 km, roughly an hour east. The Sedlec Ossuary and St Barbara's Cathedral are indoor sights, which makes this the safest November choice. See the [Prague to Kutná Hora route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-kutna-hora).

## Arriving and getting around in the low season

November is low season for leisure travel, but not for business. Trade fairs, conferences and congresses keep Prague's larger hotels busy on weekdays, and the evening rush on the roads starts earlier in the dark and wet. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is November a good time to visit Prague?**
Yes, if you prefer culture to crowds. Hotels and restaurants are easier to book, the main sights are quiet and concerts run as usual. The trade-off is grey weather and sunset around 16:15.

**How cold is Prague in November?**
Daytime highs are usually 5–8 °C early in the month and 3–5 °C by the end, with nights around 0–2 °C. The damp makes it feel colder than the numbers suggest.

**Does it snow in Prague in November?**
Occasionally in the second half of the month, but snow rarely settles in the city. Drizzle and fog are far more typical.

**Are the Christmas markets open in November 2026?**
The main markets on Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square open on Saturday 28 November 2026, so only the last weekend of the month has them.

**Is 17 November a public holiday in Prague?**
Yes. Banks and offices close and public transport runs on a holiday timetable, but large shops may open, unlike over Christmas.

**What are Prague Castle's hours in November?**
From 1 November the historic buildings open 09:00–16:00 and the castle grounds 06:00–22:00. St Vitus Cathedral opens at 12:00 on Sundays.

**Which day trips from Prague work best in November?**
Karlovy Vary, Kutná Hora and Dresden, because their main sights are open all year and the drive is 1–1.5 hours. Many smaller castles close or open only at weekends.
