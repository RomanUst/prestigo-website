Every winter, Prague becomes a gateway to the mountains. Families flying in from the UK, Scandinavia and the Middle East land at Václav Havel Airport with ski bags and boot bags and head straight for the Krkonoše, the Czech Republic's highest range, or drive on into the Austrian Alps. The transfer is the part of a ski holiday most people leave until last. It is also the part that can take half a day if it goes wrong. This guide covers **ski transfers from Prague** by private chauffeur: drive times to the main resorts, what to do with skis and luggage, how winter roads change the plan, and which weeks to book early.

## Why transfer from Prague for a ski trip?

Prague has more direct flights than any airport near the Czech mountains, and it is often cheaper to fly into than the Alpine airports in peak weeks. The problem has always been the last stretch. By public transport, Špindlerův Mlýn means a bus from Prague to Vrchlabí, then a local bus up the valley, with skis, boots and suitcases at every change. That works for a student with a backpack. It does not work for a family of five.

A private transfer turns that into one continuous journey: from the arrivals hall to the hotel door, on your schedule, with a single loading of the luggage.

## Czech resorts: the Krkonoše in about two hours

The **Krkonoše (Giant Mountains)** on the Polish border are the Czech Republic's main ski region and the easiest mountain trip from Prague.

### Špindlerův Mlýn

The best-known Czech resort, with the most extensive lift system in the country and slopes for every level. The usual route runs north-east on the **D10** towards Mladá Boleslav, then on country roads through Jičín and **Vrchlabí** and up the Labe (Elbe) valley. Expect **about two hours** from Prague Airport in normal conditions, more in fresh snow. Many hotels sit along the valley road and on the slopes of Svatý Petr, so we confirm your exact address when you book.

### Pec pod Sněžkou

At the foot of **Sněžka**, the highest peak in the Czech Republic, Pec suits families and has a cable car to the summit. The usual route is the **D11** motorway east towards Hradec Králové, then north via Trutnov. Again, allow **about two hours**.

### Harrachov and Rokytnice nad Jizerou

In the western Krkonoše, **Harrachov** is known for its ski jumps and its glassworks, and **Rokytnice** has some of the longest runs in the country. Both are reached via the D10 and Turnov, in **about two hours**.

### Jizera Mountains and Ještěd

For a shorter trip, **Liberec** is about **1.5 hours** from Prague, and the slopes of **Ještěd**, with its landmark summit tower, are on the edge of the city. See the [Prague to Liberec route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-liberec).

**A note on the final kilometres.** Some hotels in Špindlerův Mlýn and Pec sit on access roads where cars are restricted or that need chains in heavy snow. When a hotel can only be reached by its own shuttle or snowcat, we drop you at the agreed meeting point and coordinate the timing with the hotel.

## The Austrian Alps: one drive from Prague

Prague is also a practical starting point for the Austrian resorts, often closer than it looks on the map. The route runs south on the **D3** past Tábor and České Budějovice, crosses into Austria and continues on the **S10** to Linz and the **A1** towards **Salzburg**, about **4 hours** in total. See the full [Prague to Salzburg route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-salzburg).

From Salzburg, the main ski regions are a short distance further:

- **Ski amadé** (Flachau, Wagrain, Altenmarkt): roughly **4.5 hours** in total from Prague.
- **Schladming–Dachstein**: roughly **5 hours**.
- **Zell am See–Kaprun** and **Saalbach Hinterglemm**: roughly **5 to 5.5 hours**.

Austria's **Saturday changeover** matters here. On peak Saturdays, traffic towards the resorts on the A10 and around Salzburg can add an hour or more. When we can, we suggest an early start or a Friday or Sunday arrival. If your accommodation allows it, it makes the drive noticeably calmer.

## Skis, boots and suitcases: choosing the car

Ski luggage changes the maths. A ski bag is long, boot bags are bulky, and families pack for a week.

- **Mercedes-Benz V-Class**: the default ski car. Up to **6 passengers and 6 bags**. With full ski kit (a ski bag, a boot bag and a suitcase each), plan on fewer passengers per car. Tell us the exact number of ski bags when you book and we will confirm the space or suggest a second car.
- **Mercedes-Benz E-Class or S-Class**: suits a couple with suitcases and boot bags, or skiers who rent equipment at the resort.
- **Child seats** (rear-facing infant, forward-facing toddler or booster) are included at no extra cost. Tell us the children's ages.

**A useful alternative:** many resorts in both the Krkonoše and Austria rent excellent equipment. Renting skis at the resort and travelling with only boots and suitcases makes the transfer simpler and often lets a family travel in one car.

## Winter driving: how the plan changes on snow days

Mountain transfers are not the same as motorway runs, and we plan them differently.

- **Winter-equipped cars.** All our vehicles run on winter tyres in the season, and drivers carry snow chains for the mountain approaches.
- **We watch the forecast.** Before every mountain pickup, your chauffeur checks weather and road reports. If heavy snow is expected, we will suggest an earlier pickup or a different approach.
- **Slow is normal.** In fresh snow, the last 20 km up the valley can take as long as the motorway section. Built-in margins mean that is not a problem for your check-in.
- **Flight tracking works both ways.** For arrivals, the driver follows your flight. For the return, we plan the pickup from your departure time with an extra margin for mountain roads.

## Peak weeks: when to book early

Ski transfers have the sharpest demand peaks of the year. Book at least a few weeks ahead for:

- **Christmas to New Year**, the busiest week of the season in both the Czech mountains and Austria.
- **February half-term** in the UK and **winter school holidays** across Europe.
- **Czech spring holidays (jarní prázdniny)**, which rotate by region through February and March and fill the Czech resorts.
- **Saturdays throughout the season**, when most Austrian rentals change over.

If you are travelling in a group with several families, book all the cars together. That way they travel in convoy and arrive at the same time.
