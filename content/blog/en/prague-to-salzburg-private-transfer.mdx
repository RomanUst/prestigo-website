Prague and Salzburg are about 375 kilometres apart, and there is no direct train between them. The rail journey usually means a change in Linz or a long way round through Germany. That is why the **Prague to Salzburg private transfer** is one of the routes we drive most, from summer festival weekends to winter trips into the Alps. This guide covers how long the drive takes, the route south through Bohemia and over the Austrian border, why **Český Krumlov** is the stop worth planning the day around, and how to time the trip for **Advent in Salzburg**.

- **Distance:** about **375 km** from central Prague to Salzburg's old town.
- **Drive time:** around **4 hours** door to door in normal traffic, with a short coffee stop.
- **Route:** D3 south past Tábor and České Budějovice, the Dolní Dvořiště/Wullowitz border, the S10 to Linz, then the A1 west to Salzburg.

## How long does the drive from Prague to Salzburg take?

Plan on **about four hours** from a hotel in Old Town, Malá Strana or Vinohrady to a hotel in Salzburg's Altstadt or near Mirabell. Most of the drive is motorway or fast expressway. The D3 runs through the rolling farmland of South Bohemia, the S10 crosses the forested Mühlviertel into the Danube valley at Linz, and the A1 runs west along the edge of the Alps.

Three things can stretch that estimate:

- **Leaving Prague.** A Friday afternoon exit from the city can add 20–30 minutes. A mid-morning start avoids most of it.
- **The A1 west of Linz.** On winter Saturdays, when the ski resorts change over, and during the Salzburg Festival in late July and August, the A1 around Salzburg can slow. Your chauffeur checks traffic before departure and adjusts.
- **Stops.** A coffee break adds 15–20 minutes. A stop in Český Krumlov turns the transfer into a full day, as covered below.

**On the train.** Rail between Prague and Salzburg usually takes six hours or more, with at least one change. It suits travellers who pack light and stay near both main stations. A private transfer makes sense when you have luggage, children or a group, when you want to arrive at a specific hotel door, or when you want to see Český Krumlov on the way instead of making it a separate trip.

## The route: south through Bohemia and over the border

Your chauffeur leaves Prague on the **D3 motorway**, heading south past **Tábor**, the old Hussite town on its hill, and on to **České Budějovice**, the capital of South Bohemia. From there the direct route continues south to the Austrian border at **Dolní Dvořiště/Wullowitz**.

The crossing is inside the Schengen Area. Usually you will not notice it, but Austria has run temporary checks at some of its land borders in recent years, so **keep your passport or national ID** in the cabin with you rather than in the boot.

On the Austrian side the road becomes the **S10 Mühlviertel expressway**, which runs down to **Linz** on the Danube. From Linz the route joins the **A1 Westautobahn**, past Wels and the lakes of the Salzkammergut to the north of the road, and into Salzburg. The last few kilometres follow the Salzach river towards the fortress on its rock.

## Český Krumlov: the stop that makes the day

**Český Krumlov** sits about **175 km** from Prague, around two and a quarter hours into the drive, and only a short detour from the direct route. From Krumlov the road rejoins the line to the border, and Salzburg is about **2.5 to 3 hours** further on. Taking the detour therefore costs very little extra driving and gives you one of the best preserved old towns in Central Europe.

The whole historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, wrapped inside a tight bend of the Vltava. In two to three hours you can:

- climb the painted **castle tower** for the view over the red roofs and the river bend;
- cross the **Cloak Bridge**, the covered walkway that links the castle courtyards high above the town;
- walk down to the **main square** and the lanes of Latrán, then along the river to the **Church of St. Vitus**;
- take a long lunch in one of the riverside restaurants before the drive on to Austria.

The historic core is a pedestrian zone, so your chauffeur drops you as close to the old-town entrance as the rules allow and collects you from the same spot. **Your luggage stays locked in the car** the whole time. Castle interior tours run mainly from April to October and the castle gardens close in winter, but the town, the tower and the views are there all year. ### Other stops on the way

- **České Budějovice.** Its main square, Přemysl Otakar II Square, is one of the largest in Central Europe, with the Samson Fountain at its centre. The city is also home to the Budvar brewery. A 45-minute stretch of the legs fits easily.
- **Hluboká Castle.** A white neo-Gothic castle about ten minutes north of České Budějovice, often compared to Windsor. Good for a short photo stop or a longer visit in season.
- **Linz.** The Hauptplatz on the Danube and the Pöstlingberg hill above the city make a good coffee or lunch stop on the Austrian side if you take the direct route.
- **Hallstatt.** The lakeside village in the Salzkammergut is not on the way, but it can be added as an afternoon detour from the A1. It adds roughly **1.5 to 2 hours** of driving. Vehicle access to the village centre is restricted, so we drop you at the edge and you walk in.

## Advent in Salzburg: when to go and how to plan it

Salzburg is one of the great Advent cities of the German-speaking world, and late autumn is when most people plan the trip.

- **The Christkindlmarkt** fills Domplatz and Residenzplatz beneath the fortress, usually from the second half of November until 26 December. On Advent weekends, brass music plays from the cathedral and the square is at its fullest in the early evening.
- **Hellbrunn Palace** holds its own Advent market in the palace courtyard on the south side of the city, and there are smaller markets at Mirabell Square and up at the fortress.
- **The Silent Night Chapel in Oberndorf**, where "Silent Night" was first sung in 1818, is about 20 km north of Salzburg. Many visitors make the short trip on one of the December evenings.
- **Český Krumlov** has its own Advent market and a large tree on the main square, which makes the Krumlov stop even better in December.

Market dates move slightly each year, so confirm them before you book. **Weekends and the last two weeks before Christmas are the busiest**, both for hotels and for cars. If your dates fall then, reserve as early as you can. Salzburg also makes a natural stop on a longer festive trip. - **Hotels in the Altstadt and around.** The old town has restricted vehicle access. Your chauffeur drives to your hotel's entrance or the nearest permitted point and helps with the luggage.
- **Salzburg Hauptbahnhof**, if your trip continues by train to Vienna, Innsbruck or Munich.
- **Salzburg Airport (SZG)**, on the west side of the city, for an onward flight.
