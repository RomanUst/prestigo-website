From late November, Prague sits within a half-day's drive of four of the finest Christmas markets in Europe: Nuremberg's **Christkindlesmarkt**, Dresden's **Striezelmarkt** — the oldest in Germany — and the Advent markets of **Vienna** and **Salzburg**. The comfortable way to reach any of them is not a coach excursion on a fixed clock or a regional train with a change and a cold platform, but a **private chauffeur-driven car** from your Prague hotel door: mulled wine when you want it, a warm car waiting, and a late, unhurried drive home. Here is the honest 2026 guide to the markets we drive most, how far each really is, and how a private day is built around them.

## Prague Christmas markets by private car at a glance

- **Nuremberg (Christkindlesmarkt)** — ~360 km south-west through Pilsen and Bavaria, about **3 hours 30 minutes** each way. The most famous market on the list; plan a full day with 4–5 hours in the Altstadt. See the [Prague to Nuremberg route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-nuremberg).
- **Dresden (Striezelmarkt)** — ~150 km north through the Saxon Switzerland national park, about **2 hours** each way. Germany's oldest Christmas market, running since 1434, and the closest by far — a genuine day trip with time to spare. See the [Prague to Dresden route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-dresden).
- **Vienna (Christkindlmarkt am Rathausplatz)** — ~295 km south-east, about **3 hours 30 minutes** each way. Doable in a long day; better as an overnight, with the Belvedere and Schönbrunn markets added. See the [Prague to Vienna route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-vienna).
- **Salzburg (Christkindlmarkt am Dom- und Residenzplatz)** — ~305 km south-west, about **3 hours 30 minutes** each way. One of the oldest Advent markets in the German-speaking world, under the fortress. An overnight, ideally paired with a Dresden or Nuremberg stop. See the [Prague to Salzburg route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-salzburg).

Market dates shift a little each year. As a rule for 2026, the German markets open in **late November** and close on **24 December**; the Austrian markets run from **mid-November to 26 December**. Confirm the exact opening day before you book, and remember that most stalls wind down in the late afternoon on the 24th.

## Nuremberg: the market to build the trip around

If you see one Christmas market from Prague, make it Nuremberg. The **Christkindlesmarkt** on the Hauptmarkt has been held since the mid-16th century, and its rules still show it: wooden stalls under red-and-white cloth, no plastic, no piped music, and the *Nürnberger Christkind* opening the market from the gallery of the Frauenkirche church. Around it, the medieval **Altstadt** — the Kaiserburg castle, the craftsmen's courtyard of the Handwerkerhof, and the separate children's market — makes an easy afternoon on foot.

By private car it is **~360 km, about 3 hours 30 minutes** each way through Pilsen and into Bavaria on the D5/A93. That distance is exactly why the chauffeur matters here: you leave Prague after breakfast, arrive as the market fills, and — this is the point — you do not have to watch the clock for a coach departure at 4pm. Have the *Feuerzangenbowle*, buy the *Lebkuchen*, and leave when you are ready for the quiet evening run home. Nuremberg also pairs naturally with a **Regensburg** stop on the way back, a UNESCO old town with its own riverside market.

## Dresden: Germany's oldest market, and the closest

Dresden is the one that works as a true day trip. The **Striezelmarkt** on the Altmarkt has run since **1434**, which makes it the oldest documented Christmas market in Germany, and it is built around the *Stollen* — the buttery fruit loaf paraded through the city on Stollenfest, the second Advent Saturday, on a cart behind a giant ceremonial knife. Beyond the Altmarkt, Dresden spreads a further half-dozen markets through the rebuilt Baroque core: the *Advent­spiel* in the Frauenkirche square, the medieval market at the Stallhof, and the *Augustusmarkt* across the river in the Neustadt.

It is only **~150 km, about 2 hours** from Prague, up the scenic Elbe valley on the D8/A17 through the sandstone gorges of Saxon Switzerland. You can be in Dresden by mid-morning, see two or three markets, have a long lunch, and still be back in Prague for dinner — or linger for the Frauenkirche at dusk and drive home in the dark. A day trip here crosses an international border, which is where a private chauffeur pays off: no group headcount, no waiting on stragglers, and the return timed to you. ## Vienna and Salzburg: when the market is a stay, not a day

Vienna and Salzburg can both be driven in a single long day from Prague — **about 3 hours 30 minutes** each way — but the markets reward an overnight. Vienna alone has more than a dozen: the big **Christkindlmarkt am Rathausplatz** with its skating loops through the park, the aristocratic market in the forecourt of **Schloss Belvedere**, the artisan stalls at **Schönbrunn**, and the small, cand-lit *Spittelberg* market in the Biedermeier lanes. One evening is not enough.

Salzburg's **Christkindlmarkt** clusters under the fortress on Domplatz and Residenzplatz, one of the oldest in the German-speaking world, with the Advent brass playing from the cathedral steps on weekends and the Hellbrunn and Mirabell markets a short hop away. Both cities make the strongest case as part of a **multi-day private tour**: Prague – Dresden – Nuremberg – Salzburg – Vienna and home, one car and one driver throughout, each market seen in the evening when it is at its best. ## How a private chauffeur works on a Christmas-market day

A market day is not a standard point-to-point transfer, and it is run differently:

- **A set wait window at the destination.** Your driver parks near the old town and waits while you are at the market — typically several hours built into the booking — so there is a warm car to come back to, not a taxi rank in the cold.
- **Luggage and purchases stay with the car.** Coats, bags, the case of *Glühwein* mugs and the wooden nativity you did not plan to buy all go in the boot between stalls.
- **A late, flexible return.** Markets are best after dark. A private car leaves when you do — 7pm, 9pm — rather than to a fixed coach or last-train schedule.
- **One car, both ways, whole party.** A Mercedes E-Class for a couple, an S-Class for more comfort, or a V-Class van for a family or small group — a single arrangement, collected from and returned to your hotel door.
- **Border crossings are routine.** Three of the four markets are outside the Czech Republic; for a private chauffeur that is simply part of the route.

## Private driver vs the coach tour vs the train

A group coach excursion is the cheaper way to reach any of these markets, and the regional train works for Dresden. What a private chauffeur changes is the shape of the day. The coach gives you a fixed slot — often four hours, ending before the market lights come into their own — a car-park meeting point on the edge of town, and a shared pace. The train to Dresden is comfortable but ties you to the timetable and leaves you at the Hauptbahnhof with a tram ride still ahead; Nuremberg, Vienna and Salzburg all involve a change. A private car is door to door, runs to your clock, keeps the whole group together, and turns the drive itself — through the Elbe gorges, or Bohemia under snow — into part of the outing.

## Frequently asked questions

**When do the 2026 Christmas markets open?**
As a guide, the German markets (Nuremberg, Dresden) open in late November and close on 24 December; the Austrian markets (Vienna, Salzburg) run from mid-November to 26 December, with a few stalls continuing to early January. Opening dates move year to year, so confirm before booking. Weekends and the days just before Christmas are busiest.

**Can I visit more than one market in a day?**
Around Dresden, yes — you can combine the Altmarkt Striezelmarkt with the Neustadt and Frauenkirche markets easily, all on foot. Nuremberg is a full day on its own, though a Regensburg market stop on the return works. Vienna and Salzburg each have enough markets to fill an evening, so we suggest an overnight rather than trying to pair them in one day.

**How long does the driver wait at the market?**
A generous wait window is built into a market-day booking — usually several hours — so you set the pace and return to a warm car when you are ready. Longer stays and evening departures are arranged when you book.
