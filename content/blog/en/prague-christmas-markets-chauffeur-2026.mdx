From late November, Prague sits within a half-day's drive of four of the finest Christmas markets in Europe: Nuremberg's **Christkindlesmarkt**, Dresden's **Striezelmarkt** — the oldest in Germany — and the Advent markets of **Vienna** and **Salzburg**. The comfortable way to reach any of them is not a coach excursion on a fixed clock or a regional train with a change and a cold platform, but a **private chauffeur-driven car** from your Prague hotel door: mulled wine when you want it, a warm car waiting, and a late, unhurried drive home. Here is the honest 2026 guide to the markets we drive most, how far each really is, and how a private day is built around them.

An overnight, ideally paired with a Dresden or Nuremberg stop. See the [Prague to Salzburg route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-salzburg).

## Nuremberg: the market to build the trip around

If you see one Christmas market from Prague, make it Nuremberg. The **Christkindlesmarkt** on the Hauptmarkt has been held since the mid-16th century, and its rules still show it: wooden stalls under red-and-white cloth, no plastic, no piped music, and the *Nürnberger Christkind* opening the market from the gallery of the Frauenkirche church. Around it, the medieval **Altstadt** — the Kaiserburg castle, the craftsmen's courtyard of the Handwerkerhof, and the separate children's market — makes an easy afternoon on foot.

By private car it is **~295 km, about 3 hours** each way through Pilsen and into Bavaria on the D5/A6. That distance is exactly why the chauffeur matters here: you leave Prague after breakfast, arrive as the market fills, and — this is the point — you do not have to watch the clock for a coach departure at 4pm. Have the *Feuerzangenbowle*, buy the *Lebkuchen*, and leave when you are ready for the quiet evening run home. Nuremberg also pairs naturally with a **Regensburg** stop on the way back, a UNESCO old town with its own riverside market.

## Dresden Striezelmarkt: Germany's oldest Christmas market, and the closest

Dresden is the one that works as a true day trip. The **Striezelmarkt** on the Altmarkt has run since **1434**, which makes it the oldest documented Christmas market in Germany, and it is built around the *Stollen* — the buttery fruit loaf paraded through the city on Stollenfest, the second Advent Saturday, on a cart behind a giant ceremonial knife. Beyond the Altmarkt, Dresden spreads a further half-dozen markets through the rebuilt Baroque core: the *Advent­spiel* in the Frauenkirche square, the medieval market at the Stallhof, and the *Augustusmarkt* across the river in the Neustadt. In 2026 the Striezelmarkt runs from **25 November to 24 December**, and the Stollenfest parade is on **Saturday 5 December**.

It is only **~150 km, about 2 hours** from Prague, up the scenic Elbe valley on the D8/A17 through the sandstone gorges of Saxon Switzerland. You can be in Dresden by mid-morning, see two or three markets, have a long lunch, and still be back in Prague for dinner — or linger for the Frauenkirche at dusk and drive home in the dark. A day trip here crosses an international border, which is where a private chauffeur pays off: no group headcount, no waiting on stragglers, and the return timed to you. ## Vienna and Salzburg Christkindlmarkt: when the market is a stay, not a day

Vienna and Salzburg can both be driven in a single long day from Prague — **about 3.5 to 4 hours** each way — but the markets reward an overnight. Vienna alone has more than a dozen: the big **Christkindlmarkt am Rathausplatz** with its skating loops through the park, the aristocratic market in the forecourt of **Schloss Belvedere**, the artisan stalls at **Schönbrunn**, and the small, cand-lit *Spittelberg* market in the Biedermeier lanes. One evening is not enough. For 2026, the Rathausplatz Christkindlmarkt runs from **13 November to 26 December**, and Salzburg's market from **19 November 2026 to 1 January 2027**.

Salzburg's **Christkindlmarkt** clusters under the fortress on Domplatz and Residenzplatz, one of the oldest in the German-speaking world, with the Advent brass playing from the cathedral steps on weekends and the Hellbrunn and Mirabell markets a short hop away. Both cities make the strongest case as part of a **multi-day private tour**: Prague – Dresden – Nuremberg – Salzburg – Vienna and home, one car and one driver throughout, each market seen in the evening when it is at its best. ## How a private chauffeur works on a Christmas-market day

A market day is not a standard point-to-point transfer, and it is run differently:

- **A set wait window at the destination.** Your driver parks near the old town and waits while you are at the market — typically several hours built into the booking — so there is a warm car to come back to, not a taxi rank in the cold.
- **Luggage and purchases stay with the car.** Coats, bags, the case of *Glühwein* mugs and the wooden nativity you did not plan to buy all go in the boot between stalls.
- **A late, flexible return.** Markets are best after dark. A private car leaves when you do — 7pm, 9pm — rather than to a fixed coach or last-train schedule.
- **One car, both ways, whole party.** A Mercedes E-Class for a couple, an S-Class for more comfort, or a V-Class van for a family or small group — a single arrangement, collected from and returned to your hotel door.
- **Border crossings are routine.** Three of the four markets are outside the Czech Republic; for a private chauffeur that is simply part of the route.

## Private driver vs the coach tour vs the train

A group coach excursion is the cheaper way to reach any of these markets, and the regional train works for Dresden. What a private chauffeur changes is the shape of the day. The coach gives you a fixed slot — often four hours, ending before the market lights come into their own — a car-park meeting point on the edge of town, and a shared pace. The train to Dresden is comfortable but ties you to the timetable and leaves you at the Hauptbahnhof with a tram ride still ahead; Nuremberg, Vienna and Salzburg all involve a change. A private car is door to door, runs to your clock, keeps the whole group together, and turns the drive itself — through the Elbe gorges, or Bohemia under snow — into part of the outing.
