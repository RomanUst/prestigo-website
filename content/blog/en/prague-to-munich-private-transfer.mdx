Prague and Munich sit on either side of the Bohemian Forest, about 385 kilometres apart by road, yet there is still no fast direct train between them. That gap is why the **Prague to Munich private transfer** is one of the routes we drive most. This guide covers how long the drive takes, the route south-west across the Bavarian border, the stops worth making in Plzeň, Regensburg and Nuremberg, and how arrivals work in Munich, whether that is a hotel in the Altstadt, the trade-fair grounds or the airport.

## Prague to Munich at a glance

- **Distance:** about **385 km** from central Prague to central Munich.
- **Drive time:** around **4 hours** door to door in normal traffic, with a short coffee stop.
- **Route:** D5 past Plzeň, the Rozvadov/Waidhaus border, the A6 and A93 past Regensburg, then the A9 south into Munich.
- **Vehicles:** Mercedes-Benz **E-Class** or **S-Class** for up to 3 passengers, **V-Class** for up to 6 passengers and 6 bags.
- **Included:** fuel, the Czech vignette, driver time, and drop-off at any Munich address, Munich Hauptbahnhof or Munich Airport (MUC).
- **Price:** one fixed quote for the whole car, agreed before pickup. See the [Prague to Munich route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-munich).

In short: four hours with a chauffeur who collects you from your hotel and stops where you ask. It can be a straight run with one coffee, or a day in Bohemia and Bavaria with Munich at the end of it.

## How long does the drive from Prague to Munich take?

Plan on **about four hours** from a hotel in Old Town, Malá Strana or Vinohrady to a hotel in Munich's Altstadt, Maxvorstadt or Schwabing. It is motorway almost the whole way, and the drive itself is calm: a long stretch of the D5 through west Bohemia, a forested climb to the border, then the open Upper Palatinate and the Danube valley before Munich.

Three things can stretch that estimate:

- **The Munich approach.** The last 30 kilometres on the A9 and the city streets are the least predictable part of the trip. A weekday rush hour or a big event at the Allianz Arena can add 20–30 minutes.
- **The border.** The crossing at Rozvadov/Waidhaus is inside the Schengen Area, but Germany has run temporary checks at its land borders in recent years. Usually that means slowing through a checkpoint. Occasionally there is a short queue.
- **Stops.** A coffee break adds 15–20 minutes. A stop in Plzeň, Regensburg or Nuremberg turns the transfer into a half day or a full day, as covered below.

**On the train.** Rail between Prague and Munich usually takes around five and a half to six hours, often with a change, and the line is slow on the Czech side. It is a fine option if you travel light, have time and stay near both main stations. A private transfer makes sense when you have luggage, children or a group, when time matters, or when you need to arrive at a specific door: a hotel in Bogenhausen, an office in the Werksviertel or a hall at Messe München.

## The route: south-west across the Bohemian Forest

Your chauffeur leaves Prague on the **D5 motorway**, heading south-west past Beroun and the wooded valley of the Berounka. After about an hour you pass **Plzeň**, the largest city of west Bohemia. From there the D5 climbs through the hills of the **Bohemian Forest** to the border at **Rozvadov/Waidhaus**.

**Keep your passport or national ID** in the cabin with you rather than in the boot, in case of a border check.

On the German side the road becomes the **A6**. Shortly after the border, the route turns south onto the **A93** through the Upper Palatinate, reaches the Danube at **Regensburg** and continues to the **A9**, the main motorway into Munich from the north. If you are heading to the airport rather than the city, the route leaves the A9 before Munich for the A92. ## Stops worth making on the way

This is where a private transfer differs from a train ticket. The route passes three of the most attractive old towns in the region, and you decide how much of that you want.

### Plzeň: the first hour out of Prague

**Plzeň** lies about **90 km** from Prague, roughly an hour into the drive. The **Republic Square**, one of the largest in Bohemia, is framed by the Gothic **St. Bartholomew's Cathedral**, whose tower is the tallest church tower in the country. For many travellers, though, Plzeň means beer: the **Pilsner Urquell brewery** has run guided tours of its cellars for decades, and it is where pale lager was first brewed in 1842. Allow 60–90 minutes for the square and a short tour, more if you add lunch.

### Regensburg: the stop most people remember

**Regensburg** is about **285 km** from Prague, around three hours in, and just over an hour before Munich. Its medieval old town on the Danube is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the best preserved in Germany. In 90 minutes you can walk across the 12th-century **Stone Bridge**, see **St. Peter's Cathedral** with its twin Gothic spires, and wander the lanes of patrician towers that give the town its skyline. The small sausage kitchen beside the bridge has been serving travellers for centuries and is a natural lunch stop.

Your chauffeur drops you at the edge of the old town and collects you from the same spot. Your luggage stays locked in the car throughout.

### Nuremberg: the western variant

**Nuremberg** is about **295 km** from Prague and sits on a slightly different line: the A6 continues west to the city, and the A9 then runs south to Munich. Choosing Nuremberg over Regensburg adds roughly **an hour of driving** to the day. In return you get the **Imperial Castle** above the old town, the **Hauptmarkt** with the Beautiful Fountain, and the half-timbered streets around the Albrecht Dürer House. From late November until Christmas Eve, the **Christkindlesmarkt** on the Hauptmarkt is one of the most famous Christmas markets in Europe. Our [Christmas markets guide](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-christmas-markets-chauffeur-2026) covers it in more detail.

### Karlštejn: an early detour near Prague

If you want a Czech castle before you leave the country, **Karlštejn**, the Gothic castle founded by Charles IV, sits just off the D5 about 40 minutes from central Prague. Combined with the walk up to the castle and a guided tour, it needs around two hours. It works best on a full-day itinerary with an early start.

### Building a day around the transfer

A few combinations we drive often:

- **Coffee only:** about 4 hours door to door.
- **Regensburg lunch stop:** leave Prague mid-morning, spend 90 minutes on the Danube and arrive in Munich in the late afternoon.
- **Plzeň and Regensburg:** a short morning stop at the brewery or cathedral, lunch in Regensburg, Munich by early evening.
- **Nuremberg day:** leave Prague early, spend the afternoon at the castle and old town, then an easy evening run down the A9 to Munich.

## Arriving in Munich: hotels, stations, the trade fair and MUC

- **Hotels anywhere in the city.** Altstadt, Maxvorstadt, Schwabing, Bogenhausen or Nymphenburg: your chauffeur drives to the door and helps with the luggage.
- **Munich Hauptbahnhof**, if your trip continues by train to Salzburg, Innsbruck or Zurich.
- **Messe München** in Riem, in the east of the city. During large trade fairs the roads around the grounds are busy, and we plan the drop-off around your hall and badge collection.
- **Munich Airport (MUC).** It lies north-east of the city, so a direct drive from Prague never enters Munich at all. See the [Munich Airport guide](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-to-munich-airport-private-transfer) for timing.

The route also works in reverse. Your chauffeur can collect you from your Munich hotel, from the Messe or from MUC arrivals and drive you to any address in Prague.

## Which car for Prague to Munich?

Four hours is long enough for the choice of car to matter.

- **Mercedes-Benz E-Class**: up to 3 passengers and **2 large bags**. The right choice for a business traveller or a couple travelling light.
- **Mercedes-Benz S-Class**: the same capacity with a quieter, roomier rear cabin. Better if you plan to work, take calls or sleep on the way.
- **Mercedes-Benz V-Class**: up to **6 passengers and 6 bags**. The car for families, groups, trade-fair teams with sample cases, and anyone with skis or a pushchair.

**A simple rule:** if a couple has more than two large suitcases, book the V-Class. Child seats (rear-facing infant, forward-facing toddler or booster) are included at no extra cost. Tell us the children's ages when you book. A German-speaking chauffeur is available on request.

## Practical tips for the Prague–Munich transfer

- **Decide on stops before you travel.** We can adjust on the day, but a planned Regensburg or Nuremberg stop lets us set the right pickup time and suggest where to eat.
