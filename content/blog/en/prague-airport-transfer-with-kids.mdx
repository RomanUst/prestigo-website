You have been travelling since early morning, the children are tired, and there are four suitcases, a pushchair and a car seat question still to solve. This guide covers the **Prague airport transfer with kids**: which child seat to ask for, which car fits your family and your luggage, how arrival works at Václav Havel Airport, and the small details that turn the trip to your hotel into the easiest part of the day.

## Child seats: what to ask for

Children grow quickly, so the right seat depends on weight and height rather than age alone. When you book, you can request three types:

- **Infant seat** — a rear-facing carrier for newborns and babies in their first months. If you travel with your own infant carrier that clicks into a pram, you are welcome to use it instead; the chauffeur will help secure it.
- **Child seat** — a forward-facing seat with its own harness for toddlers and young children.
- **Booster cushion** — for older children who have outgrown a harnessed seat but are still too small for an adult belt alone.

The simplest approach is to give us each child's **age and approximate weight**. We match the seat to the child, and the chauffeur fits it before the drive to the airport. Up to three seats fit side by side in the rear of the V-Class, so families with three young children travel in one car.

**What Czech law says.** In the Czech Republic, children under 36 kg and shorter than 150 cm must travel in a child restraint suited to their weight and height. A pre-booked private transfer is the easiest way to be certain that the correct seat is in the car before you land, rather than hoping one is available when you arrive.

## Which car fits your family?

Luggage, not passengers, usually decides the car. A family of four fits into a sedan on paper, but a family of four with a pushchair and four large suitcases does not.

- **One parent and one child, light luggage** — **E-Class**. Two large cases and two cabin bags, and room for one child seat.
- **A couple with one child** — **E-Class** or **S-Class** if luggage is modest. Choose the S-Class for a quieter, roomier rear cabin after a long-haul flight.
- **Two adults and two or three children** — **V-Class**. Up to six passengers, up to six large suitcases plus cabin bags, and space for a folded pushchair. The rear sliding doors make it easy to lift a sleeping child in and out.
- **Grandparents travelling too, or two families together** — **V-Class**, or two cars if the luggage is heavy. For larger groups we can arrange several V-Class vehicles that travel together.
- **Skis, a travel cot or a bicycle** — mention it when you book. Bulky items change the car more than an extra passenger does.

**A simple rule:** if you have more than two large suitcases, or any pushchair at all, book the V-Class. Telling us the exact number of passengers and bags is the single detail that matters most.

## Arrival step by step

Václav Havel Airport is compact and easy to navigate with children, as long as you know where you are heading.

1. **Landing and passport control.** Flights from Schengen countries (most of Europe) arrive at **Terminal 2** and skip passport control. Flights from outside Schengen — the UK, the US, the Middle East, Asia — arrive at **Terminal 1** and pass through passport control first. With young children, ask staff about family-friendly lanes when you reach the queue.
2. **Baggage reclaim.** Collect your suitcases and your pushchair; gate-checked pushchairs usually come back at the aircraft door or at the oversized baggage counter. There is no need to rush: your driver is already tracking the flight.
3. **The arrivals hall.** Walk through customs into the public hall. Your chauffeur stands just past the exit with your name on a board. Hand over the luggage trolley — loading the car is part of the job.
4. **To the car.** The car is a short walk away, the child seats are already fitted, and the cabin is at a comfortable temperature. Children climb in, and you are on the road in minutes.

## Night flights and long-haul arrivals with children

Many long-haul flights reach Prague late in the evening or early in the morning, exactly when children are at their most tired.

- **The schedule follows your flight.** If the plane lands at 23:40 or two hours late, the chauffeur is there when you come out. No re-booking, no phone call in the baggage hall.
- **Sleeping children.** Because the seats are already fitted and adjusted, a sleeping child can be carried straight from the arrivals hall into the car and buckled in without waking.
- **A quiet drive.** The ride to central Prague takes about 25–35 minutes at night. Tell us if you would like the cabin dark and quiet, or if you need a stop at a 24-hour pharmacy on the way.

## Small details that make the difference

- **Share a phone number that works on landing.** A roaming number or a messaging app on airport Wi-Fi is enough. It is the backup if a baggage belt is slow or a pushchair goes missing.
- **Mention allergies or special needs.** Water is always in the car; if a child needs something specific, or a family member uses a wheelchair or walks slowly, tell us in advance and we plan the pickup around it.
- **Lost or delayed luggage.** If a bag does not arrive, the lost-luggage desk can take time. Your free waiting time absorbs most delays; if it runs longer, simply let the chauffeur know.
- **Hotel arrival.** In the historic centre some streets are pedestrian-only. Your chauffeur takes you to the closest legal stopping point and helps carry the bags to reception.
- **The return trip.** Book the departure transfer at the same time. The same seats are fitted again, and we recommend leaving the hotel about three hours before a long-haul flight and two and a half hours before a European one when travelling with children.
