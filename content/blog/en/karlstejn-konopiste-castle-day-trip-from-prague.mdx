Two of the most rewarding castles in Bohemia sit within an hour of Prague, on opposite sides of the Vltava valley. **Karlštejn** is the Gothic fortress Emperor Charles IV built to guard the crown jewels; **Konopiště** is the hunting château where Archduke Franz Ferdinand lived until the summer of 1914. Seen together, they tell the story of Bohemia from the medieval empire to the eve of the First World War — and with a private driver, both fit comfortably into one day. This guide covers the **Karlštejn and Konopiště day trip from Prague**: how far each castle is, how to plan a day that is full but not rushed, which tours to book, and what changes between summer and winter.

## Karlštejn and Konopiště at a glance

- **Karlštejn:** about **35 km** south-west of central Prague, roughly **40 minutes** by car.
- **Konopiště:** about **50 km** south-east of Prague, just outside Benešov, roughly **45–50 minutes** via the D1 motorway.
- **Between the two:** about an hour's drive across the Brdy foothills and the Vltava valley.
- **Ideal length:** **8–9 hours** door to door, with one guided tour at each castle and an unhurried lunch.
- **Vehicles:** Mercedes-Benz **E-Class** or **S-Class** for up to 3 passengers, **V-Class** for families and groups of up to 6.
- **Season:** both castles run their full programme from spring to October; in November the choice narrows, and Konopiště's interiors close from December to March.

The short version: start at Karlštejn in the morning, lunch on the way, Konopiště in the afternoon, back in Prague for dinner.

## Why combine the two castles?

On paper you could visit either castle alone in half a day. The reason to pair them is contrast. Karlštejn is a medieval statement of power — stone walls on a wooded hill, a residence built in tiers, each one holier than the last, with the treasury chapel at the very top. Konopiště is a lived-in nineteenth-century home: furnished rooms, a private bathroom that was cutting-edge in its day, corridors lined with hunting trophies, and a park with a rose garden. One is about an emperor; the other is about a family. Visiting both gives you five centuries in a single day, without a long drive.

There is also a practical reason. Public transport to each castle runs from a different Prague station on a different line, so doing both by train in one day is close to impossible. With a car, the transfer between them becomes a pleasant hour through villages and forest rather than a logistical puzzle.

## Karlštejn: Charles IV's treasure house

Charles IV founded **Karlštejn** in 1348 as a place to keep the imperial regalia and his collection of holy relics. The castle rises in stages above the village: the Burgrave's Palace, the Imperial Palace, the Marian Tower, and finally the Great Tower, which houses the **Chapel of the Holy Cross** — its walls set with semi-precious stones and lined with panel paintings by Master Theodoric.

**Which tour?** Karlštejn can only be visited on a guided tour, and booking ahead is strongly recommended:

- **Route I — the Imperial Residence.** The classic tour through the representative rooms of Charles IV. It runs for most of the year and is the right choice for most visitors.
- **Route II — the castle chapels**, including the Chapel of the Holy Cross. Smaller groups, a more exclusive visit, and only in the main season — it does not run from November to the end of April. Places are limited, so book well in advance.
- **Route III** is a special tour of the whole castle, available only on certain dates.

**Getting up to the castle.** Cars cannot drive through Karlštejn village. Your chauffeur drops you at the closest point at the foot of the village, and from there it is a walk of about 20 minutes uphill on a paved lane lined with cafés and shops. For guests who would rather not walk, a horse-drawn carriage or a local taxi usually runs up part of the way in season. The chauffeur waits below and collects you at the same point.

Allow **2 to 2.5 hours** at Karlštejn including the walk, the tour and a coffee in the village.

## Lunch between the castles

The drive from Karlštejn to Konopiště takes about an hour, which makes the middle of the day the natural place for lunch. There are two easy options. One is a traditional Czech lunch in Karlštejn village before you leave — game, duck or svíčková in one of the inns at the foot of the hill. The other is to drive on and eat near Konopiště, where restaurants near the château gates serve classic Bohemian cooking. Tell us your preference and we will time the day around it, or ask your chauffeur for a recommendation on the day.

## Konopiště: Franz Ferdinand's hunting château

**Konopiště** began as a Gothic fortress in the thirteenth century, but what you see today is the home Archduke **Franz Ferdinand d'Este**, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, created after buying the estate in 1887. He lived here with his wife Sophie and their children until both were assassinated in Sarajevo in June 1914 — the event that set off the First World War.

The château is famous for three things: its furnished interiors, which still feel like a private house; the archduke's vast collection of **hunting trophies**, which line the corridors in their thousands; and his collection of art and objects depicting **St George**, now shown in a museum in the former orangery.

**Which tour?** Konopiště offers several routes. The most popular are:

- **The South Wing apartments** — Baroque-furnished rooms, the hunting corridor and a historical bathroom.
- **The North Wing apartments** — including the armoury and the chapel.
- **Franz Ferdinand's private apartments** — the family's own rooms, the most personal of the tours.

Most visitors choose one tour; history enthusiasts sometimes take two. Audio guides in English, German and Russian are available on the main routes. Allow **2 to 2.5 hours** for one tour, a walk through the park to the **rose garden** and the lake, and a look at the St George museum.

### Three castles in a day?

Some itineraries add **Křivoklát**, a royal hunting castle deep in the forest west of Karlštejn. It is a beautiful place, but three guided tours plus three drives in one day leaves little time to enjoy any of them. Our honest advice: choose two. If Křivoklát appeals more than Konopiště, pair it with Karlštejn instead — the two are closer together and make an equally good day.

## Seasons and opening hours

The castles are run by the Czech National Heritage Institute and follow seasonal timetables. Hours change year to year, so always check the official sites before you travel, but the pattern for 2026 is:

- **April to October:** both castles are open most days of the week, with all main tours running; outside the peak summer weeks they are usually closed on Mondays. This is the best time for the two-castle day.
- **November:** Karlštejn opens **Friday to Sunday** with the Imperial Residence tour only; Konopiště opens at **weekends** only. A two-castle day is still possible on a Saturday or Sunday, with shorter opening hours.
- **December to March:** Konopiště's interiors close for the winter. Karlštejn keeps a reduced winter programme — Friday to Sunday, the Route I tour, closed over Christmas itself.

In winter we suggest a different shape of day: Karlštejn in the morning, a long lunch, and back to Prague for the [Christmas markets](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-christmas-markets-chauffeur-2026) in the late afternoon. In autumn, the drive between the castles through the forests of the Brdy and the Vltava valley is at its most beautiful — late September and October are our favourite weeks for this trip.

## Practical tips

- **Book the tours in advance.** Both castles sell tickets for timed guided tours, and summer slots — especially Karlštejn's chapel tour — sell out. We can suggest timings that fit the drive before you book.
- **Wear comfortable shoes.** The walk up to Karlštejn is steep in places and cobbled at the top.
- **Photography.** Interior photography is often restricted or requires a separate permit; the castle exteriors and Konopiště's park are free to photograph.
- **Take a layer.** Castle interiors stay cool even in summer.
- **Keep the day flexible.** If a tour runs long or the gardens are too lovely to leave, simply tell your chauffeur — the plan moves with you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you visit Karlštejn and Konopiště in one day from Prague?**
Yes. Karlštejn is about 40 minutes from Prague and Konopiště about 45–50 minutes, with roughly an hour between them. With a private driver, one guided tour at each castle and lunch fit comfortably into an 8–9 hour day.

**How far is Karlštejn Castle from Prague?**
About 35 km south-west of central Prague, roughly 40 minutes by car. Cars cannot enter the village, so allow about 20 minutes to walk up to the castle from the drop-off point.

**Is Konopiště open in winter?**
Konopiště's interiors are open from spring to October, at weekends in November, and closed from December to March. Karlštejn keeps a reduced winter programme from Friday to Sunday. Always check the official opening hours before you travel.

**Which Karlštejn tour should I book?**
Route I, the Imperial Residence, suits most visitors and runs for most of the year. Route II includes the Chapel of the Holy Cross, runs only in the main season and has limited places, so book it well in advance.

**Can we add Křivoklát Castle to the same day?**
It is possible but rushed. We recommend choosing two castles — Karlštejn with either Konopiště or Křivoklát — so each visit has time to breathe.
