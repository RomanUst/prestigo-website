If you are flying into Prague but staying somewhere else, the simplest plan is a **direct transfer from Prague Airport to Brno** or to whichever city is your real destination: your chauffeur meets you in the arrivals hall and drives straight onto the motorway, without a bus into the city, a train station or a second change of luggage. From the terminal, Pilsen is about an hour away, Liberec an hour and a half, Dresden and České Budějovice about two hours, and Brno and Český Krumlov two and a half. This guide gives the drive time for each city, explains which ones bypass Prague entirely and which cross it, and covers luggage, night arrivals and the return to your flight. ## Why a direct transfer beats going through the city

Václav Havel Airport has no railway station. The public route to Brno, for example, is the **Airport Express bus** to Praha hlavní nádraží (about 40 minutes), then roughly two and a half hours on the train and a taxi at the other end: three handovers with all your luggage after an overnight flight. Direct coaches keep a fixed timetable that will not wait for a delayed long-haul flight.

The airport sits on Prague's western edge, beside the **D0 ring road**, whose western and southern sections are open. Cars heading west, south or east join the motorway network within minutes of the terminal and never enter the centre. The **northern section is still not built**, as the Czech road authority does not expect construction to start before 2027, so the trip to Dresden is the one that crosses the city's northern districts.

## Arriving at PRG: from the arrivals hall to the motorway

Give us your flight number when you book. Flights from outside the Schengen area (the UK, the US, the Gulf, India, Asia) arrive at **Terminal 1**; Schengen flights arrive at **Terminal 2**. Your chauffeur tracks the flight, so an early or late landing moves the meeting time automatically, and waits **inside the arrivals hall with a name board**, not kerbside. The first 60 minutes after landing are free, which covers a slow passport queue at Terminal 1 or a late suitcase. How the greeting works in detail is in our guide to the [Prague Airport meet and greet](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-airport-meet-and-greet).

From the hall it is a short walk to the car. If you want to change money, buy a SIM card or pick up a coffee first, there is time: nobody is waiting on a timetable.

## East to Brno and Olomouc on the D1

**Brno** draws business travellers heading for trade fairs at the **Brno Exhibition Centre (BVV)** and families visiting students at the city's universities. The route takes the southern ring road to the **D1**, the country's main motorway, then runs through the Vysočina highlands past Humpolec and Jihlava. Allow 2 h 15 min to 2 h 30 min to central Brno or the exhibition grounds. Jihlava, roughly halfway, is the natural place for a coffee stop. The D1 carries heavy freight traffic, and on Friday afternoons and before holiday weekends it slows near Prague; your chauffeur checks the traffic before leaving the terminal.

**Olomouc** is about three hours away via the D46, and for **Zlín** or **Ostrava** the same car simply keeps driving; see the [Prague to Brno route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-brno) for the full corridor.

## West to Pilsen and the spa towns

The quickest onward trips from the airport go west. **Plzeň** is about one hour away on the D5, and the car never touches Prague: from the terminal it takes the D0 south to the D5 junction. More on the city, including the Pilsner Urquell brewery, is on the [Prague to Plzeň route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-plzen).

**Karlovy Vary** is even simpler, as the D6 starts almost at the airport perimeter. Spa guests heading for Karlovy Vary, Mariánské Lázně or Františkovy Lázně, usually with luggage for a two- or three-week stay, can be at their spa hotel about an hour and twenty minutes after leaving the terminal.

## North to Dresden and Liberec

**Dresden** is about two hours from the terminal. Because the northern ring road does not exist yet, the car crosses Prague's northern districts to reach the **D8**, which then runs through the České středohoří hills and the Ore Mountains into Germany, where it becomes the **A17**. There is no passport control at the Czech–German border, as both countries are in the Schengen area, but German police do run spot checks near the border, so keep your passport in your hand luggage rather than in the boot. The Czech motorway vignette is included in the transfer. See the [Prague to Dresden route](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-dresden).

**Liberec**, at the foot of the Jizera Mountains, is about an hour and a half away on the D10. Depending on the hour, your chauffeur either loops round the southern and eastern ring road or crosses the city to reach it, whichever is faster that day. In winter the cars run on winter tyres, as Czech law requires from November to March in wintry conditions; tell us about ski bags for Ještěd when you book.

## South to České Budějovice and Český Krumlov

The southern trips follow the ring road to the D1, then turn south on the **D3** at Mirošovice: about two hours from the terminal to České Budějovice. **Český Krumlov** adds another 30 minutes on a country road through the hills, so allow about 2 h 30 min. The historic centre is closed to through traffic; the car stops at the edge of the old town, and your chauffeur walks your luggage to the hotel or arranges a hand-off with hotel staff. 

## Late-night arrivals, luggage and the right car

Many long-haul flights from Asia and the Gulf land late in the evening, when trains and buses to the regions have stopped. A private car is the only way to reach Brno or Český Krumlov the same night without a hotel stop in Prague. After midnight the roads are empty and drive times are at the low end of the table.

For the car, count bags, not only passengers:

- **E-Class:** up to 3 passengers with two large suitcases and cabin bags.
- **S-Class:** the same capacity with the executive rear cabin, for a long drive when you want to rest or work.
- **V-Class:** up to 6 passengers and 6 large bags, or fewer passengers with ski bags, a pushchair or trade-fair cases.

Child seats are fitted before pickup if you tell us the children's ages; groups larger than six travel in two cars.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does it take to get from Prague Airport to Brno by car?**
About 2 h 15 min to 2 h 30 min to central Brno or the Brno Exhibition Centre. The route takes the D0 ring road to the D1 motorway and does not cross central Prague.

**Is there a train from Prague Airport to Brno?**
Not directly. The airport has no railway station, so the train means taking the Airport Express bus to Prague main station (about 40 minutes), then a train of about two and a half hours. A private car goes from the terminal to your Brno address in one trip.

**How far is Prague Airport from Pilsen?**
About 90 km, roughly one hour by car on the D5 motorway, without driving through Prague.

**Do I need my passport for a transfer from Prague Airport to Dresden?**
Yes, carry it. There is no fixed border control between Czechia and Germany, but German police make random checks near the border. Keep your passport in your hand luggage.

**What happens if my flight is delayed?**
We track the flight and adjust the meeting time. The first 60 minutes after the actual landing are free. If the flight is cancelled or diverted, we rearrange the transfer for the new arrival.

**Can we stop on the way?**

**Which car do I need for four passengers with luggage?**
A Mercedes V-Class. It takes up to six passengers and six large suitcases. The E-Class and S-Class are for up to three passengers.
