## Arriving at Prague Airport: Terminal 1 and the name board

Direct and connecting flights from Dubai, Doha, Riyadh, Jeddah, Kuwait and the rest of the region arrive from outside the Schengen area, so they use **Terminal 1** at Václav Havel Airport. That means passport control first, then baggage reclaim, then customs into the public arrivals hall.

For a large family, baggage reclaim is the slow part. Ten or twelve suitcases rarely arrive together, and someone usually has to find a second trolley. This is where a pre-booked transfer earns its place:

1. **The flight is tracked.** If you land early or two hours late, the pickup time moves with the aircraft. Nobody needs to call from the baggage hall.
2. **The wait is built in.** About 60 minutes of free waiting starts when the plane lands, which is normally enough for passport control and every suitcase.
3. **The meeting is simple.** Walk through customs and your chauffeur is standing just past the exit with your family name on a board.
4. **The luggage is handled.** Hand over the trolleys. Loading the car is the chauffeur's job, and the car is a short walk from the hall.

A practical tip: put the hotel name and one phone number that works on landing (roaming or a messaging app on airport Wi-Fi) in the booking. ## Large families and a lot of luggage: one V-Class or two cars

For families from the Gulf, the luggage usually decides the car before the number of passengers does. A sedan — the **E-Class** or **S-Class** — takes up to 3 passengers and 2 large suitcases, which suits parents travelling alone or a couple with one child. Almost every family trip needs the **V-Class**.

How to choose:

- **Up to 6 people, up to 6 large suitcases:** one V-Class. Six passengers, six large cases and six cabin bags, with sliding side doors and facing seats in the rear, so the family sits together and talks on the way.
- **Up to 6 people with a lot more luggage:** two cars. The family rides together in the V-Class; the second car carries the remaining suitcases and, if you wish, one or two passengers.
- **7 people or more:** two V-Class vans that leave the airport together and arrive at the hotel together. For larger parties we arrange several vehicles under one booking; see [group transfers](https://rideprestigo.com/services/group-transfers).
- **Grandparents or anyone who walks slowly:** mention it when you book. The chauffeur parks as close as the rules allow and the pickup is planned around the slower pace.

Count the suitcases honestly, including shopping bags you expect to bring home. On the departure day the car you need is often bigger than on arrival. ## Planning the day around meals and prayer

Prague is a compact city, and most families plan their days around three fixed points: meals, prayer times and rest for the children. A private car makes that easy, because the route can change at any moment.

- **Choose your restaurants in advance.** Prague has halal-certified and Middle Eastern restaurants; check current opening hours and certification yourself, then give the addresses to the chauffeur.
- **Build in the breaks.** With hourly hire, a return to the hotel in the middle of the day for prayer and rest costs nothing extra in planning — the car is already yours.
- **The Old Town is pedestrian.** Several central streets are closed to cars. The chauffeur stops at the closest legal point and tells you exactly where to meet again.

## A day trip to Karlovy Vary, the spa town

Karlovy Vary is the classic family day out from Prague, and well known to visitors from the Gulf: hot springs, long colonnades, grand hotels and wide promenades along the river. It lies about **130 km west of Prague**, around **1 hour 30–45 minutes** on the D6 motorway, entirely within the Czech Republic.

Why a private car suits this trip:

- **The spa centre is pedestrianised.** The colonnades and the large spa hotels sit in a zone with regulated drop-off points. Give us your hotel's name rather than a street address and the chauffeur routes to the correct entrance.
- **A stop at Loket.** Ten minutes before Karlovy Vary, Loket is a small medieval town with a castle in a bend of the Ohře river. With a private car it is simply a request.
- **Day trip or stay.** Some families spend the day and return to Prague in the evening; others stay several nights at a spa hotel. We can do a one-way transfer, a return trip with waiting, or both directions on different days.
