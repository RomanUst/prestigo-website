A **Prague main station transfer** takes 10 to 20 minutes to most central hotels. Your chauffeur meets you inside Praha hlavní nádraží with a name board, takes your cases and walks you to a car parked a few minutes away. The station is close to the centre, but it is still a long walk with luggage. The square outside is a busy main road, and the taxi rank is where visitors most often get overcharged. ## Which Prague station will your train use?

Most long-distance trains into the city end at **Praha hlavní nádraží**. These include the Railjets and RegioJet trains from **Vienna**, the EuroCity services from **Berlin, Dresden and Hamburg**, and the trains from **Budapest and Bratislava**. It is the station people mean when they say "Prague main station", and the one your ticket should name as Praha hl.n.

Two other stations matter:

- **Praha-Holešovice**, in the north of the city. Some trains from Germany and Poland stop there before the main station. Unless your hotel is in Holešovice or Letná, stay on board until Praha hl.n.
- **Praha Masarykovo nádraží**, a short walk from the main station. It mostly serves regional trains, so international visitors rarely arrive there.

Look at the final stop printed on your ticket and include it in the booking. If you arrive at Holešovice instead, the meeting point simply moves there.

## Where your chauffeur meets you inside Praha hl.n.

The station has two parts. Facing the park and Wilsonova street is the historic **Fanta building**, the Art Nouveau hall completed in 1909 with its glass dome and twin towers. Behind and below it is the modern concourse with shops, ticket offices and the entrance to **metro line C**. The platforms are above the concourse, reached by stairs, escalators and lifts from the underpasses.

On arrival day:

1. **Send us your train number** when you book, for example "RJ 72 from Wien Hbf". We check its live running before the pickup, so if the train is late, the pickup time changes with it.
2. **Your chauffeur waits inside the station** with a name board, in the main concourse below the large departures board. If you have heavy luggage, children or anyone who needs help, ask for a **platform meet** and the chauffeur will be at your carriage when the train stops.
3. **You walk to the car together.** Your chauffeur takes the cases and walks you out to the car, parked a few minutes from the hall. You never have to queue at the taxi rank, cross the main road with suitcases or look for the right exit.

There is no hurry. The chauffeur waits for you at the meeting point, so you can take your time at the station café or the ATM.

## From the station to your hotel: realistic drive times

The station is on the edge of the New Town, inside the ring of the old city. On a normal day, drive times look like this:

- **Wenceslas Square and New Town hotels:** 5 minutes. Many are walkable, but not with two large suitcases on cobblestones.
- **Old Town, around Old Town Square and Pařížská:** about 10 minutes. Much of the Old Town core is restricted to traffic, so we drive to the nearest street the car can legally reach, never further than a short walk from your door.
- **Malá Strana and Hradčany:** 15–20 minutes across the river, depending on the bridge and the time of day.
- **Vinohrady, Žižkov and Karlín:** about 10 minutes, in residential streets where most apartment rentals are.
- **Smíchov and Anděl business hotels:** 15 minutes.

Allow an extra 10 minutes on weekday evenings between 16:00 and 18:30, and during the Christmas markets in December, when the centre is busiest. Every Old Town and Malá Strana hotel is used to car arrivals and will have the bags taken in before you reach reception.

## Leaving Prague by train: when to set off

For a departing train, we work backwards from the departure time:

- **Central hotels:** leave **35–40 minutes** before departure. That is enough for the drive, a calm walk inside and finding your platform.
- **Malá Strana, Smíchov or the outskirts:** leave **45–50 minutes** before departure.
- **Trains with reserved seats and heavy luggage**, such as the Railjet to Vienna or the EuroCity to Berlin: add **10 minutes** so you can board as soon as the train is announced and stow your cases before the racks fill up.

At Praha hl.n. the platform often appears on the departures board only shortly before the train leaves. The chauffeur can walk you in, help with the cases and wait until the platform is announced. Tell us if you would like that when you book.

## Station to airport, and the train you might not need

Two other trips start or end at the main station, and both are usually easier by car.

**Main station to Prague Airport.** There is no direct train to the airport yet. The public option is the **Airport Express (AE) bus**, which takes about 35–45 minutes and works well if you travel light. A private car takes about **30–35 minutes** and stops at your terminal's departures kerb. ## Practical tips for arriving at Prague main station

- **Have some Czech crowns or a card ready, but do not change money at the station.** The exchange counters inside offer poor rates. Withdraw from a bank ATM if you need cash. Most places in Prague accept cards.
- **Ignore offers of a ride inside the hall.** Your chauffeur will be holding a board with your name and will already know your train number. If in doubt, call the number in your booking confirmation.
- **Keep valuables close.** As at any big European station, pickpockets work the concourse and escalators. A platform meet keeps your bags in sight from the moment you step off the train.
- **Night arrivals.** The last trains from Vienna and Berlin arrive late in the evening, when the station is quiet and the metro is thinning out. A pre-booked car is the simplest way to the hotel. The same applies at the airport; see our guide to [late-night arrivals in Prague](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-airport-late-night-arrivals).

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is the drive from Prague main station to Old Town?**
About 10 minutes to hotels around Old Town Square, and 5 minutes to Wenceslas Square. The car stops at the nearest street open to traffic, a short walk from your door.

**Where exactly does the driver meet me at Praha hlavní nádraží?**
Inside the station, in the main concourse below the departures board, holding a name board. On request, the chauffeur meets you on the platform at your carriage.

**What happens if my train is late?**
Nothing changes for you. We check your train's live running before pickup, and the chauffeur is there when it actually arrives.

**Is Prague main station the same as Praha hl.n.?**
Yes. Praha hl.n. is the short form of Praha hlavní nádraží, the main station. Some international trains also stop at Praha-Holešovice in the north of the city, so check the final stop on your ticket.

**How long does it take from Prague main station to the airport?**
About 30–35 minutes by car, roughly 17–18 km, to the departures kerb of Terminal 1 or 2. The Airport Express bus takes 35–45 minutes.

**How early should I leave my hotel for a train from Prague main station?**
From a central hotel, 35–40 minutes before departure. From Malá Strana or further out, 45–50 minutes. Add 10 minutes if you have heavy luggage.

**Can the chauffeur help us onto the train?**
Yes. Ask for it when you book, and the chauffeur will walk you in, wait for the platform to be announced and help with the cases up to the train.
