## Why Bohemian glass is worth a day

Glassmaking in Bohemia goes back to the Middle Ages, and by the eighteenth century Bohemian cut glass was competing with Venice across Europe. What makes it different is the material and the hand: a hard, brilliant glass that takes deep cutting and engraving, worked by blowers, cutters and painters who often trained for years in a single technique. Several of the factories you can visit still blow glass by mouth, at a furnace, in front of you.

That is the reason to go to the source rather than a shop in the Old Town. In a glassworks you see molten glass gathered on a pipe, shaped in wooden moulds, cooled, cut on wheels and engraved — and you understand why a hand-cut decanter costs what it does. The factory shops also carry the full range, including pieces that never reach Prague.

## Rückl in Nižbor: the half-day option

The **Rückl glassworks** sits on the Berounka river in Nižbor, about 8 km beyond Beroun, a straight run west from Prague along the D5 motorway. The guided tour takes about **45 minutes** and follows the glass from the furnace through blowing, painting, crackle glass and cutting. Rückl also makes the cut-crystal **Czech Lion** statuettes awarded each year by the Czech Film and Television Academy, which gives the visit a little extra story.

Tours run all year, but the schedule changes with the season and Saturday visits are limited, so a **weekday morning** is the safest slot. We can request the tour slot for you when you reserve, and your chauffeur waits at the factory while you visit and shop.

Nižbor is close enough to turn a short visit into a full, easy day. **Karlštejn** is about 25 minutes away, and the royal hunting castle of **Křivoklát** about 20 minutes up the Berounka valley. A morning at the glassworks followed by lunch and an afternoon castle tour brings you back to Prague by early evening. ## Moser in Karlovy Vary: the crystal day in spa country

**Moser** was founded in Karlovy Vary in 1857 by the engraver Ludwig Moser and became the glassmaker to the imperial court in Vienna. It is known for lead-free crystal, deep colours and engraving, and its glassworks and museum still stand on the edge of the spa town, outside the pedestrian spa zone — so the car can drop you at the door.

What to know before you book:

- **Glassworks tours run Monday to Friday**, with start times between 9:00 and 14:00. The standard tour takes about 30 minutes, plus time in the museum.
- **Smaller VIP and creative tours** (about 1.5–2 hours, up to five guests) start at 9:00 or 11:00 and go deeper into the hot shop, cutting and engraving. Some are for ages 13 and over.
- **Booking ahead is required** for every glassworks tour; groups on the standard tour are capped at ten.
- **The museum and café** are open Monday to Saturday, 9:00–16:00, so a Saturday visit gives you the collection and the shop, but not the factory floor.
- **Closed shoes** are required in the glassworks, and the factory itself is not wheelchair-accessible; the museum is.

A typical day: pickup in Prague at 8:30, Karlovy Vary by 10:00, the 11:00 glassworks tour and museum, lunch, then an afternoon on the colonnades. On the way back, the castle town of **Loket** is a twenty-minute detour. ## The North Bohemian glass trail

North of Prague, between Nový Bor and the Jizera and Krkonoše mountains, glassmaking is less a single brand than a landscape: small glassworks, engraving studios, museums and a regional "Crystal Valley". It suits travellers who have seen a big factory before and want the craft at a smaller scale.

**Nový Bor and Lindava.** The town has a dedicated **Glass Museum** and, in nearby Lindava, the **Ajeto** glassworks, now part of the lighting maker Lasvit, where the furnace stands in the middle of the glassworks tavern — you can watch the blowers over lunch. Ajeto runs weekday tours; we confirm current times when you book. **Lemberk Castle**, about 30 minutes east, makes a good afternoon stop.

**Harrachov.** The **Novosad & syn** glassworks in the Krkonoše mountains has made glass since the early eighteenth century. Tours of about 45 minutes run every day, booked online up to 14 days ahead, and the complex includes a glass museum and its own small brewery. At about two hours each way it is a full day, best in a car you can relax in — in winter it pairs naturally with a mountain stay, as in our [Czech ski transfer guide](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-ski-transfers-2026).

Nový Bor and Harrachov are about an hour and a quarter apart. Both in one day is possible but long; for most guests, one glassworks plus a castle or a mountain lunch is the better day. ## Which glass day suits you?

- **Short on time, or travelling with children:** Rückl in Nižbor, back in Prague by lunch or extended with a castle.
- **The famous name and the finest pieces:** Moser, on a weekday, combined with the colonnades of Karlovy Vary.
- **Craft, small studios and fewer visitors:** the North Bohemian trail, with one glassworks and Lemberk Castle.
- **Gift shopping before Christmas:** any of the three in November or early December; the factory shops are quietest on weekday mornings. Around Christmas and New Year some furnaces pause, so check before planning a glass day in the last week of December.

## Buying and bringing crystal home

- **Carry, don't check.** Factory shops pack purchases in boxes made for travel. Small pieces travel best in your hand luggage; larger sets are safer shipped.
- **Shipping abroad.** The larger factory shops can arrange delivery abroad — ask at the till and keep the shipping receipt with your invoice.
- **Tax-free shopping.** If you live outside the EU, ask for a tax-free form at shops that take part in a VAT refund scheme. The goods must be shown to customs, unused, when you leave the EU, so keep them accessible rather than in a checked bag. At Prague Airport, allow extra time for the customs desk before you check in.
- **Space in the car.** Tell us if you plan a larger purchase. A V-Class holds several boxed sets as well as your luggage, and your chauffeur loads them flat so nothing shifts on the motorway.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you visit a Bohemian glass factory on a day trip from Prague?**
Yes. Rückl in Nižbor is about 45–50 minutes from Prague and Moser in Karlovy Vary about 1.5 hours, so both work as day trips. Nižbor fits into half a day; Karlovy Vary or North Bohemia take a full day.

**Which glass factory is closest to Prague?**
Rückl in Nižbor, about 50 km west of Prague near Beroun. The guided tour lasts about 45 minutes and runs all year.

**Is the Moser glassworks open at weekends?**
The glassworks tours run Monday to Friday only. The Moser museum and café are also open on Saturdays, 9:00–16:00, but you will not see the furnace in action.

**Do I need to book the glassworks tour in advance?**
Yes. Moser requires advance booking for every glassworks tour, Harrachov takes online reservations up to 14 days ahead, and Rückl and Ajeto set times on request. We arrange the slot and time your pickup around it.

**Can I combine a glass factory with a castle?**
Yes. Nižbor pairs well with Karlštejn or Křivoklát, both within about 25 minutes. Moser pairs with Loket Castle, and Nový Bor with Lemberk Castle.

**Can children join a glass factory tour?**
Yes, the standard tours welcome families, and Moser offers a family ticket. Some of Moser's longer creative tours are limited to ages 13 and over, and closed shoes are required on the factory floor.

**How do I get crystal home safely?**
Carry small pieces in your hand luggage in the factory's travel packaging, and ask the shop to ship larger sets. Non-EU residents can claim a VAT refund at participating shops if the goods are shown to customs on departure.
