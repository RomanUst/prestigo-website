A **Prague–Vienna–Budapest itinerary by private chauffeur** comes down to two drives: Prague to Vienna, about 330 km and three and a half hours, and Vienna to Budapest, about 245 km and under three hours. With one car and one driver for the whole journey, both drives become sightseeing days with stops of your choosing, and your luggage never leaves the boot between hotel doors. Below are 3, 4 and 5-day plans, the drive times, and what a multi-day booking includes — or go straight to the [multi-day chauffeur builder](https://rideprestigo.com/book/multi-day) and send us your dates.

| Leg | Distance | Drive, door to door | Stops that fit the route |
|---|---|---|---|

## Why one chauffeur instead of three separate bookings

You can book each leg as a separate transfer, and many travellers do. A single multi-day booking changes three things.

**The stops become the point of the day.** A train from Prague to Vienna takes about four hours and shows you nothing but the window. The same morning in a car can include an hour in the ossuary town of Kutná Hora or a lunch among the Lednice–Valtice palaces, and still reach your Vienna hotel by late afternoon.

**The luggage stays put.** On a rail journey through three capitals you carry your cases through six stations and three taxi ranks. With one car, the bags are loaded at the Prague hotel and unloaded at the Budapest one, with Vienna in between. For families travelling with eight or ten cases, this is often the deciding reason.

**One person knows the whole plan.** Your chauffeur has your hotels, your restaurant bookings and your flight home. If you want to leave Vienna an hour later because the Kunsthistorisches Museum ran long, it is a message to the driver, not a new booking.

## Day-by-day plans: 3, 4 and 5 days

These are frameworks, not packages. Every day can be a transfer, a block of hours in a city, or a free day without the car.

### 3 days: the essential crossing

| Day | Plan | Booked as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prague hotel → Kutná Hora (St Barbara's, the Sedlec Ossuary) → Vienna | Transfer with stop |
| 2 | Vienna on foot, or the car for Schönbrunn and the Belvedere | Free day or hourly |
| 3 | Vienna → Bratislava old town for lunch → Budapest | Transfer with stop |

This version assumes you have already spent your days in Prague. It covers both capitals with one leisurely day in Vienna and arrives in Budapest by early evening.

### 4 days: room for one great detour

| Day | Plan | Booked as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prague → Český Krumlov, overnight in the old town | Transfer |
| 2 | Český Krumlov → Vienna via the southern road | Transfer |
| 3 | Vienna city day, chauffeur on call for 6 hours | Hourly |
| 4 | Vienna → Budapest | Transfer |

Český Krumlov is about 175 km and two and a half hours from Prague, and the evening after the day-trippers leave is the best time to see it. From there to Vienna is roughly three hours over the Austrian border near Dolní Dvořiště. Our [Český Krumlov day-trip guide](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-to-cesky-krumlov-private-day-trip) explains the town in detail.

### 5 days: the unhurried version

| Day | Plan | Booked as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prague → Lednice–Valtice palaces → Mikulov or Vienna | Transfer with stop |
| 2 | Vienna, free day | No car |
| 3 | Vienna, Wachau valley afternoon or evening at the opera | Hourly |
| 4 | Vienna → Pannonhalma Abbey → Győr → Budapest | Transfer with stops |
| 5 | Budapest city day: Buda Castle, the Parliament, a thermal bath | Hourly |

Five days lets you spend the evening of day one in South Moravian wine country, take the Wachau on the Danube, and still have a chauffeured day in Budapest before the flight home. Driver accommodation is included, so the car can stay with you in Budapest without a separate booking.

## The drives between the capitals, leg by leg

### Prague to Vienna

The direct route runs on the D1 to Brno, the D52 south to the Austrian border at Mikulov, and the A5 into Vienna: about 330 km and three and a half hours. Friday afternoons out of Prague can add 20 to 30 minutes, and your chauffeur checks the D1 before every departure. The [Prague to Vienna route page](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-vienna) lists the stops we drive most. If you are still weighing the car against the railjet, our [Prague–Vienna transfer vs train comparison](https://rideprestigo.com/blog/prague-vienna-transfer-vs-train) sets out both honestly.

### Vienna to Budapest

From Vienna the A4 runs east to the Hungarian border near Nickelsdorf and becomes the M1 motorway to Budapest: about 245 km and roughly two and three-quarter hours door to door. Two stops sit almost on the line. Bratislava's old town is a short detour to the north, and the Benedictine abbey of Pannonhalma, a UNESCO site founded in 996, is about 20 minutes south of Győr. Either turns the shortest leg of the trip into a full day.

### Prague to Budapest in one day

If Vienna is not on your list, the direct drive is about 535 km and five and a half hours through Brno and Bratislava. That is a long day in the car; a lunch stop in Brno or Bratislava makes it comfortable. Details are on the [Prague to Budapest route page](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-budapest).

## Chauffeur, train or flight between the three capitals

- **Train.** Prague to Vienna takes about four hours and Vienna to Budapest about two hours forty minutes on direct services. It is comfortable and good value for a couple with light bags. It does not stop where you want, and each city means a station transfer at both ends.
- **Flights.** Prague, Vienna and Budapest are too close for flying to save time once you count the airport journeys, check-in and security.
- **Chauffeur.** Door to door, stops of your choice, one place for the luggage, and the same car for a city day at either end. It is the natural choice for families, groups up to six, older travellers and anyone with heavy luggage.
