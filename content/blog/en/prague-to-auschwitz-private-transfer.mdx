## How far is Auschwitz from Prague, and what is the road like?

The Memorial is in the Polish town of Oświęcim, between Katowice and Kraków. From Prague the car takes the D1 motorway east past Brno and on to Ostrava, crosses into Poland at Věřňovice, follows the Polish A1 north for a short stretch and then leaves it for regional roads through Pszczyna. The last part is slower than the motorway, and it is the reason the journey takes nearly five hours and not four.

Czechia and Poland are both in the Schengen area, so there is normally no stop at the border. Random checks do happen. Carry your passports: you need them at the Memorial in any case.

Fuel, the Czech motorway vignette and Polish tolls are part of the quote. We plan one break of 15 to 20 minutes, usually at a service area between Brno and Ostrava, and you can ask for more.

There is a direct train from Prague to Oświęcim, but it takes more than five hours and arrives at a station about 1.5 km from the Memorial. Coaches change in Katowice. Neither fits comfortably around a timed entry.

## Same-day return or a stop on the way to Kraków?

**The day trip.** Leave Prague at about 05:30, reach the Memorial by 10:30, visit from 11:00 to 14:30, and be back at your hotel at about 20:30. That is 15 hours door to door. It suits travellers who have only one free day and are certain they want to spend it this way. Expect to be tired. Many people do not want to talk much on the way back, and a private car allows for that.

**The transfer with a stop.** If Kraków is on your itinerary, this is the better plan. You check out in Prague, the suitcases go into the car, and the Memorial becomes a stop on a journey you were making anyway. After the visit it is a little over an hour to your hotel in Kraków. The detour adds only a short distance to the direct [Prague to Kraków drive](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/prague-krakow) of 539 km and about five and a half hours.

**The overnight version.** Drive to Kraków on the first day and visit the Memorial the next morning, either with us or on one of the frequent local services from Kraków. It is the gentlest of the three, and the best one for older travellers. A [multi-day booking](https://rideprestigo.com/book/multi-day) keeps the same chauffeur with you for both days and the return to Prague.

The same stop also works in the other direction, on a [Kraków to Prague transfer](https://rideprestigo.com/routes/krakow-prague).

## Entry cards: reserve them before you book the car

Admission to the grounds is free, but every visitor needs a personalised entry card, and the cards are issued only online at **visit.auschwitz.org**. None are available at the gate. The number is limited and the summer months fill early.

You have two choices on the booking site:

- **A general tour with a Memorial educator.** It lasts about three and a half hours, covers both Auschwitz I and Auschwitz II-Birkenau, and is offered in some twenty languages, including English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Japanese and Korean. A fee is charged for the educator. Individual visitors join a shared group, and online booking closes five days before the visit. A private educator for your party alone is arranged with the Memorial by phone, well in advance.
- **A free entry card without an educator.** These must be reserved at least seven days ahead, and the number for each day is limited.

Individual cards cannot be changed or refunded, and the name on each must match the ID you show at the entrance. So the order matters: secure the cards first, then send us the entry time. We set the departure from Prague to bring you to the car park about 45 minutes before your slot. The Memorial asks visitors to arrive at least 30 minutes early for the security check.

Your chauffeur drives and waits but is not a guide and does not enter with you.

## On the day: parking, bags and the two sites

The visit begins at the Visitor Services Centre at Więźniów Oświęcimia 55, beside the Memorial's own car park. The chauffeur stops there and remains with the car.

**Bags.** The Memorial admits bags and backpacks no larger than 35 × 25 × 15 cm, roughly the size of a small handbag. Visitors who arrive by train or coach pay for a luggage locker. With a private car there is nothing to arrange. Suitcases, coats and camera bags stay locked in the vehicle, and the chauffeur stays with it.

**Two sites.** Auschwitz I, with the brick blocks and the main exhibitions, and Auschwitz II-Birkenau are 3.5 km apart. A Memorial shuttle runs between them, and guided tours use it. If you are visiting without an educator, your chauffeur can drive you to Birkenau and meet you there afterwards.

**Children.** The Memorial does not recommend the visit for children under 14. Families with younger children often divide the day. One parent visits, and the other stays in Oświęcim or goes ahead to Kraków.

**Conduct.** Dress as you would for a cemetery. Photography for private use is allowed outdoors and in most blocks without flash; it is not allowed in the room with victims' hair in Block 4 or in the basements of Block 11. Much of Birkenau is open ground, so bring a warm coat between November and March and water in summer.

## Opening hours and the winter months

The Memorial is open every day of the year except 1 January, Easter Sunday and 25 December. Entry begins at 07:30. The last entry changes by month, and visitors may stay for 90 minutes after it:

| Month | Last entry |
|---|---|
| December | 14:00 |
| January, November | 15:00 |
| February | 16:00 |
| March, October | 17:00 |
| April, May, September | 18:00 |
| June, July, August | 19:00 |

From November to February the last tours start in the early afternoon, so a day trip from Prague needs an entry between about 10:30 and 12:00. In summer a later slot is possible, but a 05:30 start still gives the easiest day. Parts of the grounds are sometimes closed for conservation work or official ceremonies. Check the [Memorial's website](https://www.auschwitz.org/en/visiting/) a few days before you travel.

## Which car for couples, families and groups

- **E-Class:** up to three passengers. The usual choice for a couple or two friends.
- **S-Class:** the same three seats with more legroom and a quieter cabin. On a day with ten hours on the road, the difference is noticeable.

## Frequently asked questions

**How far is Auschwitz from Prague?**
About 450 km by road. A private car takes roughly 4 hours 45 minutes, or five hours with a short break.

**Can we visit Auschwitz from Prague in one day?**
Yes, with an early start. Leaving at about 05:30 gives a full guided visit and a return to Prague at about 20:30. It is around ten hours of driving in total.

**Is it better to go from Prague or from Kraków?**
Kraków is only about 70 km from the Memorial. If Kraków is part of your trip, visit on the way there or the following morning.

**Do you book the Auschwitz entry cards for us?**
No. The cards are personal and tied to your ID, so each visitor reserves them at visit.auschwitz.org. Send us the entry time and we plan the drive around it.

**Where does the chauffeur wait?**
At the Memorial's car park by the Visitor Services Centre, with the car and your luggage, for the whole visit. The waiting time is included in your quote.

**Can we leave suitcases in the car?**
Yes. Only bags up to 35 × 25 × 15 cm are allowed inside the Memorial, and everything else stays locked in the car with the chauffeur.

**Is the Memorial open in winter?**
Yes, every day except 25 December and 1 January. The last entry is at 14:00 in December and 15:00 in January.

**Do we need passports?**
Yes. There are no routine border checks between Czechia and Poland, but spot checks happen, and the Memorial checks ID against the name on each entry card.
